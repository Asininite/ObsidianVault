## 🔹 Introduction to Energy & Classification of Energy Resources

**Energy**: The ability or capacity to do work. SI Unit = Joule (J).  
Forms of energy include **mechanical, electrical, chemical, nuclear, thermal, light energy** etc.

**Law of Conservation of Energy**: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another. Example: A battery converts chemical → electrical, a heater converts electrical → thermal.

### Classification of Energy Resources

Energy resources can be classified as:

1. **By Nature of Occurrence**
    
    - **Primary energy sources**: Directly obtained from nature (coal, solar, wind, hydro, crude oil).
        
    - **Secondary energy sources**: Derived from primary sources (electricity from coal, hydrogen from water electrolysis).
        
2. **By Renewability**
    
    - **Renewable**: Continuously produced, inexhaustible (solar, wind, tidal, hydro, biomass).
        
    - **Non-renewable**: Finite, exhaustible (coal, petroleum, natural gas, uranium).
        
3. **By Usage**
    
    - **Conventional (Commercial) energy sources**: Coal, oil, natural gas, hydro, nuclear.
        
    - **Non-conventional (New/Renewable) energy sources**: Solar, wind, tidal, biomass, geothermal, fuel cells, hydrogen.
        
4. **By Sector of Use**
    
    - Domestic, Agricultural, Industrial, Transportation.
        

---

### Conventional Energy Resources

- **Coal**: Major fossil fuel, used in thermal power plants, steel industry. Issues: CO₂ → global warming, SO₂ → acid rain.
    
- **Petroleum**: Fuels like petrol, diesel, LPG. Scarce in India, pollution-causing.
    
- **Natural Gas**: Cleaner fossil fuel, used for cooking, power plants. Still finite.
    
- **Hydro Power**: Uses water turbines, renewable but requires large dams (ecological impact).
    
- **Nuclear Power**: Based on fission, produces huge energy, but has waste disposal and safety issues (e.g., Chernobyl).
    

**Limitations of Conventional Energy**

- Non-renewable (finite reserves).
    
- Pollution (air, water, land).
    
- Location-specific (coal mines, oil wells).
    
- Rising cost and geopolitical issues.
    

---

### Non-Conventional Energy Resources

- **Solar Energy** (thermal, PV).
    
- **Wind Energy**.
    
- **Biomass/Biogas**.
    
- **Geothermal Energy**.
    
- **Ocean Energy** (tidal, wave, OTEC).
    
- **Hydrogen Energy**.
    
- **Fuel Cells**.
    

**Advantages**

- Renewable, inexhaustible.
    
- Pollution-free.
    
- Can be decentralized (used in rural areas).
    

**Limitations**

- Low efficiency.
    
- High initial cost.
    
- Intermittent (solar/wind not always available).
    
- Technology still developing.
    

**Comparison (Conventional vs Non-Conventional)**

|Aspect|Conventional|Non-Conventional|
|---|---|---|
|Availability|Finite|Inexhaustible|
|Pollution|High|Very low|
|Cost|Initially cheap, but rising|High initial cost, falling later|
|Technology|Mature|Still developing|
|Examples|Coal, oil, nuclear|Solar, wind, biomass|

## ✅ Exam-Oriented Questions

### 3-Mark Question

**Q1:** Differentiate between Primary and Secondary energy resources with examples.  
**Answer:**

- **Primary energy resources** are obtained directly from nature (e.g., coal, crude oil, solar energy).
    
- **Secondary energy resources** are derived from primary sources (e.g., electricity from coal, hydrogen from electrolysis of water).
    

---

### 7-Mark Question

**Q2:** Discuss the advantages and limitations of non-conventional energy resources compared to conventional resources.  
**Answer:**  
**Advantages of Non-Conventional Sources:**

1. Inexhaustible (renewable).
    
2. Pollution-free, eco-friendly.
    
3. Can be used in rural/decentralized areas.
    
4. Reduces dependence on fossil fuels.
    
5. Helps mitigate climate change.
    

**Limitations:**

1. Low efficiency (solar ~20%, wind fluctuating).
    
2. High capital cost for installation.
    
3. Storage systems required (for night/non-sunshine hours).
    
4. Location-specific (geothermal only in volcanic areas, tidal only at coasts).
    

**Comparison with Conventional Sources:**

- Conventional sources are widely used and cheaper initially but are polluting and exhaustible.
    
- Non-conventional sources are clean, renewable, but expensive and less efficient currently.
