# **REACTION TURBINES**

Reaction turbines convert **both** pressure energy **and** kinetic energy of water into mechanical work.

Unlike impulse turbines (where runner is in air), reaction turbines always operate **fully submerged** in water.

---

# ✅ **KEY FEATURES OF REACTION TURBINES (as per KTU notes)**

1. Water enters the runner with **pressure** → pressure drops across blades
    
2. Runner is **fully enclosed in a watertight casing**
    
3. Always use a **draft tube**
    
4. Suction head improves efficiency
    
5. Can be **directly coupled** to the generator (operate at higher speeds)
    
6. Used for **low to medium heads**
    
7. Very efficient under full flow, poorer under part-flow
    
8. Require more sophisticated fabrication than impulse turbines
    
9. Susceptible to **cavitation** (vapor bubbles damaging blade surfaces)
    

---

# ⭐ **TYPES OF REACTION TURBINES (as per KTU Notes)**

1. **Francis Turbine** (Radial flow reaction turbine) – Medium head
    
2. **Propeller / Kaplan Turbine** (Axial flow reaction turbine) – Low head
    

Let's learn each of them in detail.

---

# ---------------------------------------------

# **1. FRANCIS TURBINE** – _Medium head, mixed/radial flow_

---

### **Applications**

- Medium head sites (30 m – 300 m approx.)
    
- One of the most commonly used turbines worldwide
    

---

## **Construction (given in notes)**

1. **Spiral Casing / Scroll casing**
    
    - Surrounds the runner
        
    - Distributes water uniformly around the runner
        
    - Spiral shape maintains uniform velocity
        
2. **Guide Vanes / Wicket Gates**
    
    - Control water flow
        
    - Direct water tangentially into the runner
        
    - Adjustable → allows operation at varying loads
        
3. **Runner with Curved Blades**
    
    - Complex 3D shape
        
    - Water enters radially, exits axially
        
    - Converts pressure and velocity to shaft rotation
        
4. **Draft Tube**
    
    - Diverging tube at exit
        
    - Slows water → recovers pressure
        
    - Increases effective head
        
    - Always submerged
        

---

## **Working**

1. Water enters spiral casing
    
2. Guide vanes turn the flow tangentially
    
3. Water enters runner **at high pressure**, exerts lift force
    
4. Pressure energy + kinetic energy → mechanical rotation
    
5. Water exits axially into draft tube
    
6. Draft tube recovers some kinetic energy
    
7. Turbine shaft rotates → drives generator
    

---

## **Special Points**

- Converts **both** pressure + kinetic energy
    
- Excellent efficiency at medium head
    
- Runner is more complex to manufacture
    
- Susceptible to **cavitation** at high suction heads
    

---

# **2. PROPELLER & KAPLAN TURBINE** – _Low head, axial flow_

---

KTU Notes treat these together.

Kaplan = Adjustable-blade propeller turbine.

---

# **A. PROPELLER TURBINE (Fixed-blade axial flow)**

### **Construction**

- Looks like a ship propeller
    
- Water flows **axially** through the runner
    
- Runner has **fixed blades**
    
- Guide vanes (wicket gates) regulate incoming flow
    
- Nozzle-shaped casing
    
- Usually horizontal or vertical arrangement
    

### **Working**

1. Water enters through guide vanes
    
2. Enters the propeller blades axially
    
3. Pressure difference + lift force on blades → runner rotates
    
4. Water exits through draft tube
    

### **Characteristics**

- Simple construction
    
- Poor part-flow efficiency (best at 80–100% flow)
    
- Used for **low head** (typically < 30 m)
    

---

# **B. KAPLAN TURBINE (Variable-pitch propeller)**

**Most important reaction turbine in PYQs**

### **Difference from Propeller Turbine**

- Blades are **adjustable (variable pitch)**
    
- Guide vanes also adjustable
    
- Maintains high efficiency over wide range of flows
    

### **Construction**

1. **Guide vanes** (adjustable)
    
2. **Runner with movable blades**
    
3. **Hub with pitch-changing mechanism**
    
4. **Draft tube**
    
5. **Water passage (S-type, Bulb type, or Straight-through)**
    

Notes show 3 common installation designs:

- Bulb type (generator in waterproof bulb)
    
- S-type
    
- “Cross” type (with right-angle drive)
    

---

### **Working**

1. Water enters through guide vanes → swirl induced
    
2. Water hits runner blades → both lift + pressure drop produce rotation
    
3. Blade pitch adjusts automatically depending on flow
    
4. Water exits axially via draft tube
    

### **Features**

- Very high efficiency
    
- Best for **low head, high discharge**
    
- Expensive & complex (suitable for large hydro sites)
    

---

# ⭐ **COMPARISON SUMMARY (for exam)**

|Feature|Francis|Propeller|Kaplan|
|---|---|---|---|
|Head|Medium|Low|Low|
|Flow|Mixed (radial → axial)|Axial|Axial|
|Blade movement|Fixed|Fixed|Adjustable|
|Part-flow efficiency|Good|Poor|Very good|
|Fabrication|Complex|Simple|Most complex|
|Applications|Medium head plants|Small low-head plants|Large low-head plants|

---

# ⭐ **Why Reaction Turbines Can Be Directly Coupled to Generators (as per notes)**

- They rotate **faster** than impulse turbines
    
- No need for speed-increasing drives
    
- This reduces cost & improves reliability
    

---

# ⭐ **Cavitation Issue (mentioned in notes)**

Occurs because:

- Low pressure on blades causes water to vaporize
    
- Bubbles collapse → pitting, blade damage
    

Hence:

- Runner placement must avoid excessive suction head
    
- Draft tube design must be proper
    

---

# ⭐ **Part-flow Efficiency Characteristics (from notes graph)**

- Pelton & Crossflow: high at part-load
    
- Francis: poor below 50% flow
    
- Propeller: poor except near rated flow
    
- Kaplan: excellent at various flows

# **SPEED CONTROL OF TURBINES**

The turbine tends to:

- **Speed up** when the electrical load decreases, and
    
- **Slow down** when the load increases.
    

But for hydroelectric generation, the turbine **must run at constant speed** to maintain **constant generator frequency** (e.g., 50 Hz in India).

Therefore, **a governor** is used to regulate water flow into the turbine.

---

# ⭐ **HOW SPEED CONTROL IS ACHIEVED (from notes)**

Depending on the turbine type, the governor adjusts flow differently:

---

## **1️⃣ Impulse Turbines (Pelton, Turgo, Crossflow)**

Speed is controlled by **controlling jet flow**.

Governor actions include:

### **a) Changing nozzle opening (Spear Valve)**

- Moves inside the nozzle to increase/decrease jet diameter
    
- Controls water discharge without energy loss
    
- Used in Pelton / Turgo
    

### **b) Adjusting number of jets (Multi-jet Pelton)**

- Shuts off or opens individual jets
    
- Maintains speed at different flows
    

### **c) Using a deflector plate**

- Immediately diverts jet away from runner
    
- Prevents overspeed during sudden load reduction
    
- Very fast response → avoids water hammer
    

---

## **2️⃣ Reaction Turbines (Francis, Kaplan)**

Speed is controlled by regulating water passage areas.

### **a) FRANCIS TURBINE → Adjusting Guide Vanes (Wicket Gates)**

- Guide vanes rotate to control flow entering the runner
    
- Changes flow without disturbing water head
    
- Maintains efficiency over a range of loads
    

### **b) KAPLAN TURBINE → Adjusting BOTH:**

1. **Guide Vanes**
    
2. **Runner Blade Pitch**
    

This is called **Double Regulation**.

- Both vane angle and blade pitch change together
    
- Maintains constant speed AND maximum efficiency
    

This is why Kaplan turbine performs well under part-load.

---

# ⭐ **Why Speed Control is Necessary?**

1. To ensure **constant generator output frequency**
    
2. To prevent **mechanical damage** due to overspeed
    
3. To maintain **stable operation** under load variations
    
4. To protect penstock from **water hammer** during sudden flow changes
    
5. To maximize turbine **efficiency** over varying flow conditions
    

---

# ⭐ **EXAM-READY 3-MARK SUMMARY**

(Taken exactly from your notes content)

**Speed Control of Turbines:**

- Turbines speed up when load decreases and slow down when load increases.
    
- A **governor** keeps speed constant by controlling water flow.
    
- **Impulse turbines:** flow controlled by nozzle, spear valve, jet deflector, and number of jets.
    
- **Francis turbines:** flow controlled by changing guide vane opening.
    
- **Kaplan turbines:** both guide vanes AND runner blade pitch are adjusted.

# ✅ **3-MARK ANSWER (Highly Descriptive)**

### **Classify hydraulic turbines.**

Hydraulic turbines are classified based on the following criteria:

1. **Based on the Action of Water:**
    
    - **Impulse Turbines:** Water strikes the runner in the form of a high-velocity jet at atmospheric pressure. Pressure energy is first converted fully into kinetic energy in a nozzle.  
        _Example: Pelton, Turgo, Crossflow._
        
    - **Reaction Turbines:** Runner remains fully submerged, and water undergoes a pressure drop while flowing through the blades. Both pressure and kinetic energy are converted into mechanical energy.  
        _Example: Francis, Kaplan._
        
2. **Based on Direction of Flow:**
    
    - **Tangential flow:** Pelton
        
    - **Radial flow:** Traditional Francis
        
    - **Axial flow:** Kaplan, Propeller
        
    - **Mixed flow:** Modern Francis (radial entry → axial exit)
        
3. **Based on Available Head:**
    
    - **High head (>75 m):** Pelton
        
    - **Medium head (30–75 m):** Francis
        
    - **Low head (<30 m):** Kaplan/Propeller
        

These classifications help select the correct turbine for a hydropower site.

# ⭐ **2) SUITABILITY OF TURBINES — 3-MARK ANSWER (Descriptive)**

### **Explain the suitability of Pelton, Francis, and Kaplan turbines.**

- **Pelton Turbine (Impulse):**  
    Best suited for **very high heads** (above 75 m, up to 1000 m) and **low discharge** streams. It uses a high-velocity jet to strike buckets, making it ideal for steep mountain rivers.
    
- **Francis Turbine (Reaction):**  
    Suitable for **medium head** (30–75 m). It uses mixed flow and gives high efficiency at moderate discharge, typically used in canal drops and storage projects.
    
- **Kaplan Turbine (Reaction):**  
    Ideal for **low head (<30 m)** and **high discharge** rivers. Adjustable pitch blades allow high efficiency even when flow varies, making it suitable for run-of-river schemes.

# ✅ **7-MARK: Classification of Hydraulic Turbines**

Hydraulic turbines can be classified in several ways, each relating to a different aspect of turbine operation or design. A complete classification is as follows:

### **1. Based on Energy Conversion Principle**

#### **a) Impulse Turbines**

- Water jet strikes the turbine buckets at atmospheric pressure.
    
- Entire pressure energy converts to kinetic energy in the nozzle.
    
- Runner buckets merely change the momentum of water.
    
- Works efficiently even under part-flow conditions.
    
- _Examples:_ Pelton, Turgo, Crossflow.
    

#### **b) Reaction Turbines**

- Runner is completely submerged.
    
- Pressure energy decreases as water flows through blades.
    
- Uses suction head and draft tube for energy recovery.
    
- _Examples:_ Francis, Kaplan, Propeller.
    

---

### **2. Based on Direction of Flow Through Runner**

1. **Tangential Flow:** Pelton
    
2. **Radial Flow:** Francis (traditional)
    
3. **Axial Flow:** Kaplan/Propeller
    
4. **Mixed Flow:** Modern Francis combines both radial and axial flow
    

---

### **3. Based on Available Head**

1. **High head (>75 m):** Pelton
    
2. **Medium head (30–75 m):** Francis
    
3. **Low head (<30 m):** Kaplan, Propeller
    

---

### **4. Based on Specific Speed (Ns)**

Specific speed indicates the shape and speed characteristics of turbines.

- **Low Ns (8–50):** Pelton → High head, low discharge
    
- **Medium Ns (51–225):** Francis → Medium head
    
- **High Ns (250–850):** Kaplan/Propeller → Low head, high discharge
    

---

### **5. Based on Type of Runner Construction**

- **Horizontal-shaft turbines**
    
- **Vertical-shaft turbines**
    

---

### **Why This Classification Matters**

These classifications allow engineers to choose the appropriate turbine for a site depending on available head, discharge, efficiency, cavitation limits, structural simplicity, and cost.

---

# ⭐ **7-MARK: Suitability of Turbines (Head–Discharge–Specific Speed)**

Choosing the correct turbine is crucial for maximizing efficiency of a hydropower plant. The suitability depends mainly on **head**, **discharge**, and **specific speed**.

### **1. Pelton Turbine (Impulse)**

- **Specific Speed:** 8–50
    
- **Head Range:** Very high (above 75 m, up to 1000 m)
    
- **Discharge:** Low
    
- **Reason for suitability:**  
    High head produces very high jet velocity (√2gH).  
    Buckets efficiently convert momentum change into mechanical power.  
    Pelton turbines maintain high efficiency even under part-load, making them ideal for mountain river streams.
    

---

### **2. Francis Turbine (Reaction – Mixed Flow)**

- **Specific Speed:** 51–225
    
- **Head Range:** Medium (30–75 m)
    
- **Discharge:** Moderate
    
- **Reason for suitability:**  
    Combines radial entry and axial exit, making it suitable for a broad head range.  
    Spiral casing uniformly distributes water.  
    Wicket gates regulate flow, keeping high efficiency over moderate flow variations.  
    Ideal for canal drops and storage-based power schemes.
    

---

### **3. Kaplan Turbine (Reaction – Axial Flow)**

- **Specific Speed:** 250–850
    
- **Head Range:** Low (<30 m)
    
- **Discharge:** Very high
    
- **Reason for suitability:**  
    Adjustable pitch blades allow optimal blade angle for any flow condition.  
    High discharge rivers provide the large volumetric flow needed.  
    Draft tube increases usable head by recovering kinetic energy.  
    Ideal for run-of-river schemes and low-head hydro.
    

---

### **Cavitation and Suction Head Influence**

- Reaction turbines use **draft tubes** to recover pressure, but excessive suction head leads to cavitation.
    
- Pelton turbines avoid cavitation since the runner works in air.

# **Explain the Classification and Suitability of Hydraulic Turbines. (10/14 marks)**

Hydraulic turbines are devices that convert the potential and kinetic energy of water into mechanical power. Their classification and suitability depend on their operating principle, available head, discharge, and specific speed. The detailed explanation is as follows:

---

# **1. Classification of Hydraulic Turbines**

### **A. Based on the Principle of Operation**

#### **1. Impulse Turbines**

- Operate entirely on **kinetic energy** of water.
    
- Water is accelerated through a nozzle before striking the runner.
    
- Runner operates under **atmospheric pressure**.
    
- No draft tube is needed.
    
- Suitable for high-head conditions.
    
- Examples: **Pelton**, Turgo, Crossflow.
    

#### **2. Reaction Turbines**

- Operate on a combination of **pressure** and **kinetic** energy.
    
- Runner is fully submerged in water.
    
- Pressure drops continuously across the runner.
    
- Require a **draft tube** to recover pressure energy.
    
- Examples: **Francis**, **Kaplan**, Propeller.
    

---

### **B. Based on Direction of Flow**

1. **Tangential flow:** Pelton
    
2. **Radial flow:** Traditional Francis
    
3. **Axial flow:** Kaplan and Propeller
    
4. **Mixed flow:** Modern Francis runners
    
5. **Diagonal flow:** Some advanced Francis designs
    

---

### **C. Based on Available Head**

- **High Head (>75 m):** Pelton
    
- **Medium Head (30–75 m):** Francis
    
- **Low Head (<30 m):** Kaplan, Propeller
    

This classification directly determines turbine choice.

---

### **D. Based on Specific Speed**

Specific speed (Ns) is a dimensionless parameter that compares turbine performance and helps decide turbine type.

|Turbine|Specific Speed (Ns)|Suitable Head|
|---|---|---|
|Pelton|8–50|High head|
|Francis|51–225|Medium head|
|Kaplan / Propeller|250–850|Low head|

This table is essential for turbine selection.

---

# **2. Suitability of Turbines**

### **1. Pelton Turbine (Impulse) – High Head, Low Discharge**

- Best suited for steep mountainous rivers with **very high head** and **small water flow**.
    
- Water jet velocity is proportional to √H; hence high heads produce very high jet energy.
    
- Buckets split and deflect the jet almost 180°, maximizing momentum change and mechanical output.
    
- Excellent efficiency even at part-flow.
    

---

### **2. Francis Turbine (Reaction, Mixed Flow) – Medium Head**

- Suitable for **moderate head** and discharge.
    
- Water enters radially and exits axially, enabling efficient utilization of medium heads.
    
- Adjustable guide vanes (wicket gates) control flow and maintain good efficiency across moderate load ranges.
    
- Common in small and medium hydro stations.
    

---

### **3. Kaplan Turbine (Reaction, Axial Flow) – Low Head, High Discharge**

- Used for **low-head (<30 m)** but **large discharge rivers**.
    
- Runner blades are adjustable, allowing high efficiency even under fluctuating flows.
    
- Axial flow design minimizes losses under low head.
    
- Requires a draft tube for energy recovery.
    
- Excellent for run-of-river and canal-based projects.
    

---

### **3. Influence of Cavitation, Suction, and Draft Tube**

- Reaction turbines rely on suction head but are limited by cavitation risk.
    
- Cavitation causes pitting on blades, hence turbine level must be chosen carefully.
    
- Pelton turbines are cavitation-free because the runner is in air.
    

---

### **4. Summary**

Turbine selection must consider:

- **Head**
    
- **Discharge**
    
- **Specific speed**
    
- **Cavitation limits**
    
- **Efficiency characteristics**
    
- **Cost and mechanical complexity**
    

Thus a well-chosen turbine ensures maximum efficiency and long, reliable operation of hydroelectric plants.
