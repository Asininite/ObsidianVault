# **REACTION TURBINES**

Reaction turbines convert **both** pressure energy **and** kinetic energy of water into mechanical work.

Unlike impulse turbines (where runner is in air), reaction turbines always operate **fully submerged** in water.

---

# ✅ **KEY FEATURES OF REACTION TURBINES (as per KTU notes)**

1. Water enters the runner with **pressure** → pressure drops across blades
    
2. Runner is **fully enclosed in a watertight casing**
    
3. Always use a **draft tube**
    
4. Suction head improves efficiency
    
5. Can be **directly coupled** to the generator (operate at higher speeds)
    
6. Used for **low to medium heads**
    
7. Very efficient under full flow, poorer under part-flow
    
8. Require more sophisticated fabrication than impulse turbines
    
9. Susceptible to **cavitation** (vapor bubbles damaging blade surfaces)
    

---

# ⭐ **TYPES OF REACTION TURBINES (as per KTU Notes)**

1. **Francis Turbine** (Radial flow reaction turbine) – Medium head
    
2. **Propeller / Kaplan Turbine** (Axial flow reaction turbine) – Low head
    

Let's learn each of them in detail.

---

# ---------------------------------------------

# **1. FRANCIS TURBINE** – _Medium head, mixed/radial flow_

---

### **Applications**

- Medium head sites (30 m – 300 m approx.)
    
- One of the most commonly used turbines worldwide
    

---

## **Construction (given in notes)**

1. **Spiral Casing / Scroll casing**
    
    - Surrounds the runner
        
    - Distributes water uniformly around the runner
        
    - Spiral shape maintains uniform velocity
        
2. **Guide Vanes / Wicket Gates**
    
    - Control water flow
        
    - Direct water tangentially into the runner
        
    - Adjustable → allows operation at varying loads
        
3. **Runner with Curved Blades**
    
    - Complex 3D shape
        
    - Water enters radially, exits axially
        
    - Converts pressure and velocity to shaft rotation
        
4. **Draft Tube**
    
    - Diverging tube at exit
        
    - Slows water → recovers pressure
        
    - Increases effective head
        
    - Always submerged
        

---

## **Working**

1. Water enters spiral casing
    
2. Guide vanes turn the flow tangentially
    
3. Water enters runner **at high pressure**, exerts lift force
    
4. Pressure energy + kinetic energy → mechanical rotation
    
5. Water exits axially into draft tube
    
6. Draft tube recovers some kinetic energy
    
7. Turbine shaft rotates → drives generator
    

---

## **Special Points**

- Converts **both** pressure + kinetic energy
    
- Excellent efficiency at medium head
    
- Runner is more complex to manufacture
    
- Susceptible to **cavitation** at high suction heads
    

---

# ----------------------------------------------

# **2. PROPELLER & KAPLAN TURBINE** – _Low head, axial flow_

---

KTU Notes treat these together.

Kaplan = Adjustable-blade propeller turbine.

---

# **A. PROPELLER TURBINE (Fixed-blade axial flow)**

### **Construction**

- Looks like a ship propeller
    
- Water flows **axially** through the runner
    
- Runner has **fixed blades**
    
- Guide vanes (wicket gates) regulate incoming flow
    
- Nozzle-shaped casing
    
- Usually horizontal or vertical arrangement
    

### **Working**

1. Water enters through guide vanes
    
2. Enters the propeller blades axially
    
3. Pressure difference + lift force on blades → runner rotates
    
4. Water exits through draft tube
    

### **Characteristics**

- Simple construction
    
- Poor part-flow efficiency (best at 80–100% flow)
    
- Used for **low head** (typically < 30 m)
    

---

# **B. KAPLAN TURBINE (Variable-pitch propeller)**

**Most important reaction turbine in PYQs**

### **Difference from Propeller Turbine**

- Blades are **adjustable (variable pitch)**
    
- Guide vanes also adjustable
    
- Maintains high efficiency over wide range of flows
    

### **Construction**

1. **Guide vanes** (adjustable)
    
2. **Runner with movable blades**
    
3. **Hub with pitch-changing mechanism**
    
4. **Draft tube**
    
5. **Water passage (S-type, Bulb type, or Straight-through)**
    

Notes show 3 common installation designs:

- Bulb type (generator in waterproof bulb)
    
- S-type
    
- “Cross” type (with right-angle drive)
    

---

### **Working**

1. Water enters through guide vanes → swirl induced
    
2. Water hits runner blades → both lift + pressure drop produce rotation
    
3. Blade pitch adjusts automatically depending on flow
    
4. Water exits axially via draft tube
    

### **Features**

- Very high efficiency
    
- Best for **low head, high discharge**
    
- Expensive & complex (suitable for large hydro sites)
    

---

# ⭐ **COMPARISON SUMMARY (for exam)**

|Feature|Francis|Propeller|Kaplan|
|---|---|---|---|
|Head|Medium|Low|Low|
|Flow|Mixed (radial → axial)|Axial|Axial|
|Blade movement|Fixed|Fixed|Adjustable|
|Part-flow efficiency|Good|Poor|Very good|
|Fabrication|Complex|Simple|Most complex|
|Applications|Medium head plants|Small low-head plants|Large low-head plants|

---

# ⭐ **Why Reaction Turbines Can Be Directly Coupled to Generators (as per notes)**

- They rotate **faster** than impulse turbines
    
- No need for speed-increasing drives
    
- This reduces cost & improves reliability
    

---

# ⭐ **Cavitation Issue (mentioned in notes)**

Occurs because:

- Low pressure on blades causes water to vaporize
    
- Bubbles collapse → pitting, blade damage
    

Hence:

- Runner placement must avoid excessive suction head
    
- Draft tube design must be proper
    

---

# ⭐ **Part-flow Efficiency Characteristics (from notes graph)**

- Pelton & Crossflow: high at part-load
    
- Francis: poor below 50% flow
    
- Propeller: poor except near rated flow
    
- Kaplan: excellent at various flows

# **SPEED CONTROL OF TURBINES**

The turbine tends to:

- **Speed up** when the electrical load decreases, and
    
- **Slow down** when the load increases.
    

But for hydroelectric generation, the turbine **must run at constant speed** to maintain **constant generator frequency** (e.g., 50 Hz in India).

Therefore, **a governor** is used to regulate water flow into the turbine.

---

# ⭐ **HOW SPEED CONTROL IS ACHIEVED (from notes)**

Depending on the turbine type, the governor adjusts flow differently:

---

## **1️⃣ Impulse Turbines (Pelton, Turgo, Crossflow)**

Speed is controlled by **controlling jet flow**.

Governor actions include:

### **a) Changing nozzle opening (Spear Valve)**

- Moves inside the nozzle to increase/decrease jet diameter
    
- Controls water discharge without energy loss
    
- Used in Pelton / Turgo
    

### **b) Adjusting number of jets (Multi-jet Pelton)**

- Shuts off or opens individual jets
    
- Maintains speed at different flows
    

### **c) Using a deflector plate**

- Immediately diverts jet away from runner
    
- Prevents overspeed during sudden load reduction
    
- Very fast response → avoids water hammer
    

---

## **2️⃣ Reaction Turbines (Francis, Kaplan)**

Speed is controlled by regulating water passage areas.

### **a) FRANCIS TURBINE → Adjusting Guide Vanes (Wicket Gates)**

- Guide vanes rotate to control flow entering the runner
    
- Changes flow without disturbing water head
    
- Maintains efficiency over a range of loads
    

### **b) KAPLAN TURBINE → Adjusting BOTH:**

1. **Guide Vanes**
    
2. **Runner Blade Pitch**
    

This is called **Double Regulation**.

- Both vane angle and blade pitch change together
    
- Maintains constant speed AND maximum efficiency
    

This is why Kaplan turbine performs well under part-load.

---

# ⭐ **Why Speed Control is Necessary?**

1. To ensure **constant generator output frequency**
    
2. To prevent **mechanical damage** due to overspeed
    
3. To maintain **stable operation** under load variations
    
4. To protect penstock from **water hammer** during sudden flow changes
    
5. To maximize turbine **efficiency** over varying flow conditions
    

---

# ⭐ **EXAM-READY 3-MARK SUMMARY**

(Taken exactly from your notes content)

**Speed Control of Turbines:**

- Turbines speed up when load decreases and slow down when load increases.
    
- A **governor** keeps speed constant by controlling water flow.
    
- **Impulse turbines:** flow controlled by nozzle, spear valve, jet deflector, and number of jets.
    
- **Francis turbines:** flow controlled by changing guide vane opening.
    
- **Kaplan turbines:** both guide vanes AND runner blade pitch are adjusted.
