# **1. Classification of Small Hydropower Plants**

Small hydropower plants are classified **based on both capacity and head**.

### **A. Based on Installed Capacity**

This is the most common KTU classification:

1. **Micro Hydro** – up to **100 kW**
    
2. **Mini Hydro** – **100 kW to 2 MW**
    
3. **Small Hydro** – **2 MW to 25 MW**
    

(Some references use 10 MW upper limit, but your notes use 25 MW.)

---

### **B. Based on Available Head**

1. **Low Head Plants** – **below 30 m**
    
    - Use Kaplan or Propeller turbines
        
    - Large flow required
        
2. **Medium Head Plants** – **30–100 m**
    
    - Use Francis turbines
        
3. **High Head Plants** – **above 100 m**
    
    - Use Pelton (impulse) turbines
        

---

### **C. Based on Water Flow Regulation**

1. **Run-of-river plants**
    
    - No significant storage
        
    - Water directly used as it comes
        
2. **Run-of-river with pondage**
    
    - Small pond; short-term storage (few hours)
        
3. **Reservoir type**
    
    - Long-term storage
        
    - More flexible generation
        

These are the classifications examiners expect.

# **2. Define Head and Discharge**

These two terms determine the **power** available from a hydro plant:

  
$$P = \rho g Q H  $$


Where Q = discharge, H = head.

### **Head (H)**

Head is the **vertical height difference** between:

- water level at the intake  
    and
    
- water level at the turbine outlet (tailrace)
    

It represents the **potential energy** available.

Types of head:

- Gross head
    
- Net head (after subtracting losses in conduit and penstock)
    

---

### **Discharge (Q)**

Discharge is the **volume of water flowing per second** through the turbine.


$$Q = \text{m}^3/\text{s}  $$

Higher discharge ⇒ higher power output.  
Head and discharge together determine which turbine type is suitable.

---

# **3. Define Specific Speed**

Specific speed is a **dimensionless index** that helps in **selecting the proper turbine type** for given head and discharge.

It is defined as:


$$N_s = N \sqrt{P} / H^{5/4}  $$

Where:

- **Ns** = specific speed
    
- **N** = speed of turbine (rpm)
    
- **P** = power (kW)
    
- **H** = net head (m)
    

### **Purpose**

• Determines the _most suitable turbine_ for given site conditions.  
• Low Ns → Pelton  
• Medium Ns → Francis  
• High Ns → Kaplan/Propeller

### **Typical Ranges**

- **Pelton (Impulse):** 10–60
    
- **Francis:** 60–300
    
- **Kaplan/Propeller:** 300–1000+
    

So specific speed is a turbine-selection tool.

# **4. Risks Involved in Hydrogen Storage**

Hydrogen storage has safety challenges because hydrogen is unique.

### **Major Risks:**

1. **High flammability & explosion risk**
    
    - Hydrogen has a very low ignition energy
        
    - Leaks can ignite easily
        
2. **Leakage risk**
    
    - Hydrogen molecules are the smallest among gases
        
    - Can seep through tiny gaps
        
3. **Embrittlement of metals**
    
    - Hydrogen weakens metals over time
        
    - Causes cracks/leaks in storage tanks
        
4. **High-pressure hazards**
    
    - Hydrogen cylinders usually operate at 350–700 bar
        
    - Potential for rupture if mishandled
        
5. **Wide flammability range**
    
    - Hydrogen burns in the range **4%–75%** in air
        
    - Makes accidental ignition more likely
        
6. **Invisible flame**
    
    - Hydrogen flame is colorless
        
    - Difficult to detect visually

# **1. Components and Layout of a Small Hydroelectric Plant**

A small hydro plant converts the potential energy of water into electricity using hydraulic and electrical components.

### **Main Components**

### **(1) Intake Structure**

- Located at the river or canal.
    
- Screens remove leaves and debris.
    
- Guides water into the system.
    

### **(2) Diversion Weir / Barrage**

- Slightly raises water level.
    
- Diverts water into intake without large storage.
    

### **(3) Desilting Tank**

- Settles sand, silt, and suspended particles.
    
- Prevents turbine erosion.
    

### **(4) Water Conductor System**

Includes:

- Headrace channel
    
- Culverts
    
- Tunnels
    
- Flumes  
    Used to carry water with minimal head losses.
    

### **(5) Forebay Tank**

- Balancing reservoir before penstock.
    
- Provides steady water supply.
    
- Has trash racks and spillway.
    

### **(6) Penstock**

- Steel or RCC pipe carrying water to turbine.
    
- High-pressure conduit.
    
- Needs support blocks and anchor blocks.
    

### **(7) Powerhouse**

Contains:

- Turbine
    
- Generator
    
- Control equipment
    
- Governor system
    
- Draft tube (reaction turbines)
    

### **(8) Tailrace Channel**

- Conveys discharged water safely back to river.
    
- Designed to avoid backwater effects.
    

### **Layout Explanation**

Stream → Diversion weir → Intake → Desilting tank → Headrace → Forebay → Penstock → Turbine in powerhouse → Tailrace → River.

# **2. Types of Turbines Used in Small Hydro Applications**

Turbine choice depends on **head**, **discharge**, and **specific speed**.

### **(1) Pelton Turbine (Impulse)**

- Used for **high head (above 200 m), low discharge**.
    
- Water jet strikes buckets on runner.
    
- No draft tube required.
    

### **(2) Turgo Impulse Turbine**

- Medium to high head.
    
- Water jet hits runner at angle.
    
- Higher flow handling than Pelton.
    

### **(3) Cross-flow (Banki–Mitchell) Turbine**

- Medium head, medium flow.
    
- Simple, robust, easy maintenance.
    
- Water flows through the runner twice.
    

### **(4) Francis Turbine**

- **Medium head (30–100 m)**.
    
- Mixed-flow reaction turbine.
    
- Used widely in small hydro.
    

### **(5) Kaplan / Propeller Turbine**

- **Low head (2–30 m)**.
    
- Axial-flow reaction type.
    
- Kaplan has adjustable blades → high efficiency.

# **3. Compare Impulse and Reaction Turbines**

|Feature|Impulse Turbine|Reaction Turbine|
|---|---|---|
|Principle|Works only on **kinetic energy** of water jet|Works on **pressure + kinetic energy**|
|Water Pressure at Runner|Atmospheric|Varies (higher to lower across runner)|
|Location|High head, low discharge|Low to medium head, high discharge|
|Casing|Used only to prevent splash|Pressure-tight casing required|
|Efficiency|High for high head|High for low head|
|Draft Tube|Not required|Essential|
|Example|Pelton, Turgo, Cross-flow|Kaplan, Francis|

# **4. Construction and Operation of Kaplan Turbine**

### **Construction**

1. **Scroll Casing**
    
    - Spiral-shaped, distributes water evenly.
        
2. **Guide Vanes (Wicket Gates)**
    
    - Control flow rate and direction.
        
    - Adjustable.
        
3. **Runner with Variable Pitch Blades**
    
    - Axial-flow runner.
        
    - Blades can change pitch to maintain efficiency at varying loads.
        
4. **Draft Tube**
    
    - Expands gradually.
        
    - Converts kinetic energy into pressure energy.
        
    - Improves efficiency.
        
5. **Shaft and Generator**
    
    - Vertical shaft drives generator above.
        

### **Operation**

- Water enters scroll casing and flows through adjustable guide vanes.
    
- Guide vanes direct water at optimum angle onto runner blades.
    
- Runner blades also adjust pitch → maintain high efficiency even at part load.
    
- Water exits through draft tube, recovering additional energy.
    
- Suitable for **low head and high discharge** sites.

# **5. Methods of Producing Hydrogen**

### **(1) Steam Methane Reforming (SMR)**

- Natural gas + steam → hydrogen + CO + CO₂
    
- Most common method industrially.
    

### **(2) Water Electrolysis**

- Electricity splits water into hydrogen + oxygen.  
    Types:
    
- Alkaline electrolysis
    
- PEM electrolysis
    
- High-temperature electrolysis
    

### **(3) Coal Gasification**

- Coal + oxygen + steam → syngas → hydrogen extracted.
    

### **(4) Biomass Gasification**

- Agricultural/urban biomass converted into producer gas (H₂-rich).
    

### **(5) Photolysis (Photoelectrochemical)**

- Solar energy used to split water.
    

### **(6) Biological Methods**

- Algae or bacteria produce hydrogen naturally.

# **6. Hydrogen Storage Techniques**

### **(1) Compressed Gas Storage**

- Stored in cylinders at 350–700 bar.
    
- Simple but requires strong tanks.
    

### **(2) Liquid Hydrogen Storage**

- Stored at −253°C in cryogenic tanks.
    
- Higher density but expensive and energy-intensive.
    

### **(3) Metal Hydride Storage**

- Metals absorb hydrogen into lattice.
    
- Safe but heavy and costly.
    

### **(4) Chemical Storage**

- Hydrogen stored in chemical compounds (e.g., ammonia, methanol).
    
- Released through chemical reactions.
    

### **(5) Underground/Geological Storage**

- Salt caverns, depleted oil reservoirs.
    
- Suitable for large-scale seasonal storage.

# **7. Working Principle of PEM Fuel Cell**

### **Construction**

- **Anode**: Hydrogen inlet
    
- **Cathode**: Oxygen (air) inlet
    
- **Electrolyte**: Proton Exchange Membrane (permeable only to H⁺)
    
- **Catalyst**: Platinum (speeds up reactions)
    

### **Working**

1. **At Anode:**  
    [  
    H_2 \rightarrow 2H^+ + 2e^-  
    ]
    
    - Hydrogen splits into protons and electrons.
        
2. **Protons** pass through PEM.
    
3. **Electrons** travel through external circuit (giving DC power).
    
4. **At Cathode:**  
    [  
    O_2 + 4H^+ + 4e^- \rightarrow 2H_2O  
    ]
    
    - Oxygen combines with protons and electrons → water.
        

### **Output**

- Clean electricity
    
- Heat
    
- Water vapor (only emission)

# **8. Working of Other Fuel Cells (Alkaline, MCFC, SOFC)**

### **(A) Alkaline Fuel Cell (AFC)**

- Electrolyte: KOH aqueous solution
    
- Operates at 60–90°C
    
- High efficiency
    
- Used in spacecraft
    

Reactions:  
Anode: ( 2H_2 + 4OH^- → 4H_2O + 4e^- )  
Cathode: ( O_2 + 2H_2O + 4e^- → 4OH^- )

---

### **(B) Molten Carbonate Fuel Cell (MCFC)**

- Electrolyte: Molten carbonate salts
    
- Operating temp: ~650°C
    
- Fuel: H₂, CO, natural gas
    

Reactions:  
Anode: ( H_2 + CO_3^{2-} → H_2O + CO_2 + 2e^- )  
Cathode: ( O_2 + CO_2 + 2e^- → CO_3^{2-} )

Uses:

- MW-scale power plants
    
- High efficiency + cogeneration
    

---

### **(C) Solid Oxide Fuel Cell (SOFC)**

- Electrolyte: Solid ceramic oxide
    
- Operating temp: 800–1000°C
    
- Fuel flexible (H₂, CO, methane, biogas)
    

Reactions:  
Cathode: ( O_2 + 4e^- → 2O^{2-} )  
Anode: ( H_2 + O^{2-} → H_2O + 2e^- )

Advantages:

- Very high efficiency
    
- Fuel flexible
    
- Suitable for stationary power generation
