### **Wind Turbine Components**

A **Wind Energy Conversion System (WECS)** is the complete system that converts the kinetic energy of the wind into electrical energy1111. At its heart is the wind turbine. Let's break down its main components.

#### **1. Rotor Assembly**

The rotor is the primary part of the turbine that extracts energy from the wind2. It consists of the blades and the hub3.

- **Blades**: These are the most critical parts that capture wind energy4.
    
    - They have a streamlined, airfoil-shaped cross-section designed to produce high lift and low drag forces5555.
        
    - They are typically made of high-density wood or composites of glass fiber and epoxy6.
        
    - The blades are slightly twisted from the root (where they attach to the hub) to the outer tip to reduce the tendency to stall7.
        
    - A typical modern rotor in the megawatt range can have a diameter of around 100 meters8.
        
- **Hub**: This is the central solid part of the rotor wheel to which all the blades are attached9999. The mechanism for pitch angle control is often provided inside the hub10.
    

#### **2. Nacelle**

The nacelle is the cover housing mounted at the top of the tower11111111. It contains all the generating components of the wind turbine12121212.

- **Gearbox**: The rotor turns at a relatively low speed. The gearbox is a mechanical interface that steps up the rotational speed of the shaft to the higher speed required by the generator131313.
    
- **Generator**: This is the component that converts the rotary mechanical energy from the shaft into electrical energy14.
    
- **Brake**: A brake assembly is used to stop the rotor when power generation is not desired or for safety during extreme wind conditions15.
    
- **Main Shaft**: There are typically two shafts: a low-speed shaft that connects the rotor to the gearbox and a high-speed shaft that connects the gearbox to the generator16161616.
    

#### **3. Tower**

The tower supports the nacelle and rotor at a significant height17.

- For medium and large turbines, the tower is slightly taller than the rotor diameter18.
    
- For small turbines, the tower is much larger than the rotor diameter because air is more erratic at lower heights19.
    
- Towers are made of steel or concrete and can be of tubular or lattice construction20.
    

#### **4. Control Systems**

Modern turbines use sophisticated control systems to manage operation and maximize energy output21.

- **Pitch Control**: This system controls the pitch angle (the angle between the wind direction and the plane of the blades) by turning the blades at their root222222222222222222. The system continuously adjusts the pitch to achieve optimal performance23.
    
- **Yaw Control**: This mechanism orients or steers the turbine's axis to keep the rotor facing directly into the wind24242424. This maximizes the energy captured25. While small turbines may use a passive tail vane, large turbines use an active yaw control system with motors and wind direction sensors26.

### **Classification of Wind Turbines**

Wind turbines are classified based on several criteria, primarily the orientation of their axis and the type of rotor they use27272727.

#### **1. Based on Orientation of the Rotor Axis**

- **Horizontal Axis Wind Machines (HAWT)**:
    
    - This is the most common type of wind turbine, where the rotor axis is horizontal, or parallel to the ground28282828.
        
    - The design is complex, as components like the rotor, transmission, and generator must function effectively together29.
        
    - They must be oriented to face the direction of the wind to generate power, which requires a yaw mechanism30303030.
        
    - **Advantages**: They capture more power for a given tower height and are less noisy compared to vertical axis machines31313131.
        
    - **Disadvantages**: They have a more complex design and require a yaw mechanism32323232.
        
- **Vertical Axis Wind Machines (VAWT)**:
    
    - In these machines, the rotor axis is vertical, or perpendicular to the ground33333333.
        
    - A major advantage is that their operation is independent of wind direction, so they do not need to be turned into the wind343434343434343434. This eliminates the need for a complex yaw mechanism35353535.
        
    - Heavy components like the gearbox and generator can be located at ground level, which simplifies installation, maintenance, and structural support36363636363636363636363636363636.
        
    - **Advantages**: Less complex design, no yaw mechanism needed, and easier maintenance37373737.
        
    - **Disadvantages**: They capture less power, are generally noisier, and can suffer from fatigue effects38383838.
        

#### **2. Based on the Type of Rotor**

- **Propeller Type (HAWT)**:
    
    - These are the most commonly used wind turbines for power generation39.
        
    - They typically have two or three blades for economic reasons and are rated from 1 to 3 MW40404040.
        
    - The two-blade design is highly efficient but can face vibration issues during yawing41.
        
- **Multiblade Type (HAWT)**:
    
    - These turbines have many blades, giving them high solidity (ratio of blade area to swept area)42.
        
    - They have a high starting torque, making them well-suited for mechanical work like pumping water43.
        
    - They are less efficient for electricity generation due to interference between the blades but are less noisy44.
        
- **Savonius Type (VAWT)**:
    
    - This is a drag-type machine that consists of a hollow cylinder sliced in half, with the halves mounted on a vertical shaft with a gap in between45.
        
    - Torque is produced by the pressure difference between the concave and convex sides facing the wind46.
        
    - **Advantages**: Low cost, operates at low wind velocity, and requires no yaw or pitch control47.
        
    - **Applications**: Useful for grinding grains and pumping water48.
        
- **Darrieus Type (VAWT)**:
    
    - This is a lift-type machine shaped like an "egg beater," with two or three blades that have an airfoil cross-section49.
        
    - **Characteristics**: It has high speed and high efficiency but is not self-starting and requires an auxiliary starter50.
        
    - **Advantages**: The generator and gearbox can be placed on the ground, and it does not need a yaw mechanism51.
