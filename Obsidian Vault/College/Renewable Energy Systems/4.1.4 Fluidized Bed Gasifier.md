# ⭐ **FLUIDIZED BED GASIFIER (Very High Detail)**

A **fluidized bed gasifier** is an advanced gasifier in which **air/steam is passed upward through a bed of inert particles (sand/ash)** at high velocity, causing the bed to behave like a **boiling fluid**.

Because of this fluid-like behavior, the biomass particles:

- **Float (suspend) in the hot bed**
    
- Mix thoroughly with the bed material
    
- React uniformly
    
- Undergo rapid heating and conversion
    

This makes fluidized bed gasifiers **highly efficient**, **tar-reduced**, and suitable for **large-scale gasification**.

---

# ⭐ WHY IS IT CALLED “FLUIDIZED”?

Because the solid bed (sand/ash) behaves like a **liquid** when gas velocity reaches the **minimum fluidization velocity (Umf)**.

When this happens:

- The bed particles lift and move like boiling water
    
- Biomass particles are evenly distributed
    
- Temperature is uniform across the reactor
    
- Heat transfer is extremely high
    

This makes reaction rates very fast.

---

# ⭐ **TYPES OF FLUIDIZED BED GASIFIERS**

Your notes indirectly refer to the two main types:

### ✔ 1) **Bubbling Fluidized Bed Gasifier (BFB)**

- Gas velocity slightly above minimum fluidization
    
- Bed behaves like boiling water
    
- Bubbles form and rise
    
- Good mixing
    
- Uniform temperature (~750–900°C)
    

### ✔ 2) **Circulating Fluidized Bed Gasifier (CFB)**

- Higher gas velocity
    
- Bed particles get carried out and recycled back using a cyclone separator
    
- Excellent mixing
    
- High throughput
    
- Best for **large-scale** biomass gasification (>10 MW)

# ⭐ **CONSTRUCTION OF A FLUIDIZED BED GASIFIER**

Typical components:

1. **Reactor vessel (vertical cylindrical chamber)**
    
2. **Distributor plate**
    
    - At bottom
        
    - Uniformly distributes air/steam
        
3. **Bed material**
    
    - Sand/ash/olivine
        
    - Provides heat & support
        
4. **Biomass feeding system**
    
    - Screw feeder or pneumatic feed
        
5. **Primary air/steam inlet**
    
6. **Cyclone separator** (in CFB)
    
    - Separates entrained solids
        
7. **Gas outlet** (top)
    
8. **Ash discharge system**

# ⭐ **WORKING OF A FLUIDIZED BED GASIFIER**

Fluidized bed gasifiers operate based on **excellent mixing** and **uniform temperature profiles**.

Let’s break it down clearly.

---

## 🔥 **1. Start-Up**

- Bed material (sand) is heated to **~750–900°C**
    
- Air or steam is blown from the bottom through the distributor plate
    

When the gas velocity reaches the **fluidization velocity**, the bed becomes fluid-like.

---

## 🔥 **2. Biomass Feeding**

Biomass (chips, pellets, sawdust) is fed into the hot bed.

Because the particles are **small and mixed**, drying and pyrolysis happen almost instantly.

---

## 🔥 **3. Main Reaction Zones in a Fluidized Bed**

Unlike fixed bed gasifiers (Updraft/Downdraft), **fluidized beds don’t have distinct layers**.  
Instead, **all reactions occur simultaneously** due to intense mixing.

### ✔ **Drying:**

Moisture evaporates rapidly due to excellent heat transfer.

### ✔ **Pyrolysis:**

Biomass decomposes → produces volatiles, tar, char.

### ✔ **Oxidation:**

Limited air partially burns char & volatiles → generates heat.

### ✔ **Reduction:**

Reactions produce producer gas:

#### Boudouard

[  
C + CO_2 \rightarrow 2CO  
]

#### Water–Gas

[  
C + H_2O \rightarrow CO + H_2  
]

#### Shift Reaction

[  
CO + H_2O \rightarrow CO_2 + H_2  
]

#### Methanation

[  
C + 2H_2 \rightarrow CH_4  
]

**Because char is well-distributed**, these reactions occur uniformly throughout the bed.

---

## 🔥 **4. Gas–Solids Separation (for CFB)**

Since gas velocity is high, some bed particles get carried away and are separated by:

- **Cyclone separator**
    
- Solids returned to reactor → maintains circulating bed
    

---

## 🔥 **5. Producer Gas Output**

Product gas exits from the **top** and typically contains:

- CO
    
- H₂
    
- CH₄
    
- CO₂
    
- Steam
    
- Lower tar than updraft (but higher than downdraft)
    

---

# ⭐ **ADVANTAGES OF FLUIDIZED BED GASIFIER**

### ✔ **1. Very high heat & mass transfer**

Fast reaction rates → high efficiency.

### ✔ **2. Handles wide variety of fuels**

Can gasify:

- Rice husk
    
- Sawdust
    
- Bagasse
    
- Agro-residues
    
- Industrial biomass waste
    
- High-ash fuels
    

Better than fixed-bed gasifiers.

### ✔ **3. Uniform temperature distribution**

Avoids hot spots → prevents ash melting.

### ✔ **4. Higher throughput**

Best for **medium and large-scale** gasification.

### ✔ **5. Better tar cracking than updraft**

Although not as low-tar as downdraft, tar is significantly lower than fixed beds.

### ✔ **6. Continuous and stable operation**

---

# ⭐ **DISADVANTAGES**

1. More complex than fixed-bed gasifiers
    
2. Requires **small, uniform biomass particles**
    
3. Higher particulate content in product gas
    
4. Tar level not as low as downdraft gasifier
    
5. Erosion of bed material and reactor walls possible
    
6. Needs careful control of air/steam velocity
    

---

# ⭐ **APPLICATIONS**

- Power generation (1–20 MW+)
    
- Industrial heating
    
- Cogeneration
    
- Gas engines with cleaning
    
- Large-scale biomass gasification systems

# ⭐ **EXAM-READY 7-MARK ANSWER (Write this)**

**A fluidized bed gasifier is an advanced biomass gasifier in which air or steam is blown upward through a bed of inert material such as sand causing it to behave like a bubbling fluid. Biomass particles fed into the bed become suspended and undergo rapid drying, pyrolysis, oxidation, and reduction due to excellent mixing and uniform temperatures of 750–900°C. The main reactions include Boudouard, water–gas, shift, and methanation reactions producing CO, H₂, and CH₄. The product gas is taken from the top. Fluidized bed gasifiers can be bubbling or circulating types, depending on gas velocity. They offer high efficiency, uniform temperature, fast reaction rates, and ability to gasify a wide range of biomass, making them suitable for medium and large-scale power generation. Disadvantages include higher complexity, need for uniform small particles, and higher particulate load in the gas.**
