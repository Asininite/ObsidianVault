# ⭐ **BIOMASS CONVERSION TECHNOLOGIES (Very High Detail)**

Biomass conversion technologies are the methods used to convert **raw biomass** (wood, crop residue, manure, waste) into **useful forms of energy** such as **heat, electricity, gases, liquid fuels, and solid fuels**.

Your notes classify these technologies into **three major groups**:

# ⭐ 1. **PHYSICAL CONVERSION TECHNOLOGIES**

# ⭐ 2. **THERMO-CHEMICAL CONVERSION TECHNOLOGIES**

# ⭐ 3. **BIO-CHEMICAL CONVERSION TECHNOLOGIES**

Let's study each with **deep detail**, mechanisms, products, diagrams (text), and exam points.

---


# ⭐ **1. PHYSICAL CONVERSION TECHNOLOGIES**

# ================================================

These processes **do NOT alter the chemical structure** of biomass.  
They simply **improve handling, storage, density, or usability**.


---

## ✔ **1.1 Briquetting**
- Biomass residues (sawdust, rice husk, straw) are compressed under high pressure.
- Lignin in biomass acts as a **natural binder**, so no chemicals needed.
- Moisture content must be 10–15%.
- Briquettes are uniform, dense, and burn longer.    

**Advantages:**
- Increased energy density
- Smoke-free combustion
- Easy transport
- Used in boilers and domestic stoves
---

## ✔ **1.2 Pelletization**
- Biomass is ground into powder → forced through **die holes** to form small cylindrical pellets.
- Pellets have **low moisture**, **high calorific value**, **high density**.
- Used in pellet stoves, small boilers, co-firing in power plants.
---

## ✔ **1.3 Oil Extraction / Expelling**

- Seeds like Jatropha, Karanja, Sunflower, Soybean are pressed to extract **vegetable oil**.
    
- Oil is used for **biodiesel production** via transesterification.
    

---

# ⭐ Summary

Physical processes → **density increased, moisture reduced, transport/storage improved** → **useful for combustion/gasification/biochemical stages**.

# ⭐ **2. THERMO-CHEMICAL CONVERSION TECHNOLOGIES** (Most Important)

# ==================================================

These processes use **heat + limited or no oxygen** to break biomass into gases, liquids, or solid products.

Your notes include 4 major thermo-chemical technologies:

1. **Combustion (Incineration)**
    
2. **Pyrolysis**
    
3. **Gasification**
    
4. **Liquefaction**
    

Let’s study each in **deep detail**.

---

# ⭐ **2.1 Direct Combustion (Incineration)**

Biomass + Excess Air → Heat + Flue Gas + Ash

### ✔ Process:

- Biomass is burned in **excess oxygen**.
    
- Heat released is used to:
    
    - Produce steam → turbines → electricity
        
    - Industrial heating
        
    - Domestic cooking/heating
        

### ✔ Applications:

- Boilers
    
- Furnaces
    
- Waste incinerators
    
- Biomass steam power plants
    

### ✔ Disadvantages:

- Low efficiency (~20%)
    
- High emission if biomass is wet
    
- Requires fuel preparation
    

---

# ⭐ **2.2 Pyrolysis (VERY IMPORTANT)**

Pyrolysis = **Thermal decomposition of biomass in the absence of oxygen**  
Temp: **400°C – 800°C**

### ✔ Products:

1. **Bio-char** (solid) – charcoal
    
2. **Bio-oil** (liquid) – tarry, energy-dense
    
3. **Syngas** (gas) – CO, H₂, CH₄
    

### ✔ Types of Pyrolysis:

1. **Slow Pyrolysis**
    
    - Low heating rate
        
    - High char, low oil
        
2. **Fast Pyrolysis**
    
    - High heating rate, short residence time
        
    - **High liquid yield (~60–70%)**
        
3. **Flash Pyrolysis**
    
    - Very fast heating
        
    - High gas yield
        

### ✔ Uses:

- Bio-oil → boiler fuel, upgraded to diesel
    
- Bio-char → soil conditioner + activated carbon
    
- Syngas → gas engines / heating
    

---

# ⭐ **2.3 Gasification (Most Important for Exams)**

Gasification = Conversion of biomass to **producer gas** by heating in **limited oxygen**.

Producer gas contains:  
CO + H₂ + CH₄ + CO₂ + N₂

You already learned the 4 zones:

- Drying
    
- Pyrolysis
    
- Oxidation
    
- Reduction
    

### ✔ Applications:

- IC engines (dual fuel)
    
- Small power plants
    
- Boilers
    
- Furnaces
    

---

# ⭐ **2.4 Liquefaction**

Conversion of solid biomass → **bio-oil** using **high pressure + moderate temperature** and sometimes catalysts.

### ✔ Conditions:

- Temp: **250–300°C**
    
- Pressure: **5–20 MPa**
    
- Solvent: Water or organic solvents
    

### ✔ Output:

- Heavy, viscous liquid called **bio-crude**
    
- Can be refined into:  
    ● Diesel-like fuels  
    ● Chemicals  
    ● Synthetic fuels
    

Used for **wet biomass** (sewage sludge, algae), because pyrolysis needs dryness.

# ⭐ **3. BIO-CHEMICAL CONVERSION TECHNOLOGIES**

# ==================================================

These rely on **microorganisms** to convert biomass into useful fuels.

Your notes list:

- **Anaerobic digestion → Biogas**
    
- **Fermentation → Ethanol**
    
- **Esterification → Biodiesel**
    

Let’s cover each clearly.

---

# ⭐ **3.1 Anaerobic Digestion (Biogas Production)**

Biomass → (No Oxygen) → Biogas (CH₄ + CO₂)

### ✔ Four stages:

1. **Hydrolysis** – Biomass → Simple sugars
    
2. **Acidogenesis** – Sugars → Organic acids
    
3. **Acetogenesis** – Acids → Acetate + H₂
    
4. **Methanogenesis** – Acetate + H₂ → **Methane**
    

### ✔ Biogas composition:

- **55–65% CH₄**
    
- **30–40% CO₂**
    
- Trace gases: H₂S, NH₃
    

### ✔ Applications:

- Cooking fuel
    
- Electricity generation
    
- Organic fertilizer from slurry
    

---

# ⭐ **3.2 Ethanol Production (Fermentation)**

Feedstocks:

- Sugar crops (sugarcane)
    
- Starch crops (corn, wheat)
    
- Cellulosic biomass (wood, bagasse)
    

### ✔ Steps:

1. **Pretreatment**
    
2. **Hydrolysis** – Polysaccharides → glucose
    
3. **Fermentation** – Yeast converts glucose → ethanol
    
4. **Distillation** – Purify ethanol
    

### ✔ Uses:

- Blended with petrol (E10, E20)
    
- Chemical industry
    
- Gasoline substitute
    

---

# ⭐ **3.3 Biodiesel Production (Transesterification)**

Vegetable oils + Alcohol → Biodiesel + Glycerol  
Catalyst: NaOH / KOH

Biodiesel = **fatty acid methyl ester** (FAME)

### ✔ Uses:

- Used in diesel engines
    
- Renewable and cleaner
    

---

# ⭐ **WHY MULTIPLE CONVERSION TECHNOLOGIES ARE NEEDED?**

Your notes emphasize these reasons:

1. Biomass exists in **solid, liquid, gaseous** forms.
    
2. Different applications need different fuel types.
    
3. Raw biomass has **low energy density**, so conversion improves usability.
    
4. Thermo-chemical methods produce **gases & liquids** suitable for engines.
    
5. Biochemical methods produce **clean fuels** (biogas, ethanol, biodiesel).

# ⭐ **FULL 7-MARK EXAM ANSWER (Ready to write)**

Biomass conversion technologies are classified into physical, thermo-chemical, and biochemical methods. Physical methods such as briquetting, pelletization, and oil expelling improve the density, handling, and usability of biomass without changing its chemical composition. Thermo-chemical methods include direct combustion (complete oxidation to produce heat), pyrolysis (thermal decomposition in absence of oxygen to produce bio-oil, bio-char and syngas), gasification (partial oxidation in limited air to produce producer gas containing CO, H₂ and CH₄), and liquefaction (conversion of wet biomass into bio-crude at high pressure and moderate temperature). Biochemical methods depend on microorganisms and include anaerobic digestion producing biogas in four stages, fermentation for converting sugars and starches into ethanol, and transesterification of vegetable oils into biodiesel. Together, these technologies enable biomass to be converted into solid, liquid, and gaseous fuels suitable for heat, electricity generation, engines, and transportation applications.
