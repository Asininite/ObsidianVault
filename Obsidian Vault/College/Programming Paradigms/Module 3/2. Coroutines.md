### 1. What is a Coroutine?** (For the 3-Mark "Short Note") A coroutine is a special kind of subprogram. If you are asked to write a short note, you must include these defining characteristics:

- **Multiple Entry Points:** Unlike normal subprograms, coroutines can have multiple entry points, which are controlled by the coroutines themselves.
    
- **History Sensitive:** They have the ability to maintain their status (variable values and execution state) between activations. Because of this, coroutines must be history sensitive and therefore require static local variables.
    
- **Resume vs. Call:** Because secondary executions of a coroutine often begin at points other than its very beginning, the invocation of a coroutine is called a **resume** rather than a _call_.
    
- **Quasi-Concurrency:** Only one coroutine is actually in execution at a given time. However, because they partially execute, transfer control, and then restart right where they left off, they appear to run concurrently while sharing a single processor. This interleaved execution is known as quasi-concurrency.
    

### **2. Differentiating Coroutines from Conventional Subprograms (For the 6-Mark Question)** Since you already know the characteristics of a standard subprogram (from our previous prerequisite review), answering the 6-mark comparison question is just a matter of contrasting the two. You can write this as a direct comparison or a table:

- **Entry Points:**
    
    - _Conventional Subprogram:_ Has only a single entry point (execution always starts at the very beginning of the code block).
        
    - _Coroutine:_ Has multiple entry points controlled by `resume` statements.
        
- **Execution Flow & Control:**
    
    - _Conventional Subprogram:_ The caller is suspended, the subprogram runs completely to its end, and control strictly returns to the caller.
        
    - _Coroutine:_ A coroutine often partially executes, pauses to transfer control to some other coroutine, and when restarted, resumes execution just after the statement it used to transfer control.
        
- **Invocation Mechanism:**
    
    - _Conventional Subprogram:_ Invoked using a standard `call` instruction.
        
    - _Coroutine:_ Invoked using a `resume` instruction, because it doesn't always start from the top.
        
- **State Retention (Memory):**
    
    - _Conventional Subprogram:_ Typically uses stack-dynamic local variables, meaning it "forgets" its state and data once it finishes executing and returns.
        
    - _Coroutine:_ Must be history sensitive to remember where it paused. Therefore, it relies on static local variables to maintain its status between different activations.
