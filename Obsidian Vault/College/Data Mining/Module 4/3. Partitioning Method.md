### 1. What is the Partitioning Algorithm?

The Partitioning Algorithm is a method used in Association Rule Mining to find frequent itemsets.

**How it removes the disadvantage of Apriori:** The standard Apriori algorithm requires scanning the entire database multiple times (once for each itemset size: 1-itemsets, 2-itemsets, etc.), which is incredibly slow and I/O heavy for massive databases.

The Partitioning Algorithm completely solves this by requiring **exactly two database scans**, significantly reducing the I/O bottleneck.

**The Core Principle:**

> _"If an itemset is globally frequent in the entire database, it MUST be locally frequent in at least one of the partitions."_

---

### 2. The Two-Phase Working Mechanism

**Phase 1: Local Frequent Itemset Generation (Scan 1)**

1. Divide the massive database into $n$ non-overlapping partitions. Each partition must be small enough to fit entirely into the computer's main memory.
    
2. For each partition, find all the _Local Frequent Itemsets_. To do this, calculate a **Local Minimum Support** = (Global Minimum Support Percentage $\times$ Number of transactions in that specific partition).
    
3. Combine all the local frequent itemsets from all partitions to form a set of **Global Candidate Itemsets**.
    

**Phase 2: Global Frequent Itemset Generation (Scan 2)**

1. Scan the _entire_ database for a second and final time.
    
2. Count the actual global frequency for every itemset in your "Global Candidate Itemsets" list.
    
3. If a candidate's global count meets or exceeds the Global Minimum Support, it is declared a **Global Frequent Itemset**.

### 3. Step-by-Step Example (KTU Exam Style)

Let's trace a simple example you can easily memorize and recreate on paper.

**The Setup:**

Assume a database with 6 transactions and a **Global Minimum Support of 50%** (i.e., an itemset must appear in at least $6 \times 0.5 = 3$ transactions to be frequent).

|**TID**|**Items Bought**|
|---|---|
|T1|A, B, C|
|T2|A, B|
|T3|A, C|
|T4|A, B, C|
|T5|B, C|
|T6|A, B|

#### **Phase 1: Divide and Conquer (Scan 1)**

We will divide this database into 2 partitions of 3 transactions each.

_Local Minimum Support_ = 50% of 3 transactions = **1.5 (Round up to 2)**. So, an itemset needs a count of 2 within its partition to be locally frequent.

**Partition 1 (T1, T2, T3):**

- Items: T1(A,B,C), T2(A,B), T3(A,C)
    
- Item Frequencies: A=3, B=2, C=2. _(All are $\ge 2$, so all are local frequent 1-itemsets)_
    
- Pair Frequencies: {A,B}=2, {A,C}=2, {B,C}=1. _(Only {A,B} and {A,C} are $\ge 2$)_
    
- **Local Frequent Sets for P1:** `{A}, {B}, {C}, {A,B}, {A,C}`
    

**Partition 2 (T4, T5, T6):**

- Items: T4(A,B,C), T5(B,C), T6(A,B)
    
- Item Frequencies: A=2, B=3, C=2. _(All are $\ge 2$, so all are local frequent 1-itemsets)_
    
- Pair Frequencies: {A,B}=2, {A,C}=1, {B,C}=2. _(Only {A,B} and {B,C} are $\ge 2$)_
    
- **Local Frequent Sets for P2:** `{A}, {B}, {C}, {A,B}, {B,C}`
    

**Generate Global Candidates:**

Take the Union of the local frequent sets from P1 and P2:

`Candidates = {A}, {B}, {C}, {A,B}, {A,C}, {B,C}`

#### **Phase 2: Global Counting (Scan 2)**

Now, we scan the whole database of 6 transactions to check the actual counts of our Candidates. Remember, the **Global Min Support is 3**.

- Count(A) = 5 **(Frequent)**
    
- Count(B) = 5 **(Frequent)**
    
- Count(C) = 4 **(Frequent)**
    
- Count({A,B}) = 4 **(Frequent)**
    
- Count({A,C}) = 3 **(Frequent)**
    
- Count({B,C}) = 2 _(Not Frequent - Reject!)_
    

**Final Output:**

The Global Frequent Itemsets are: `{A}, {B}, {C}, {A,B}, {A,C}`.
