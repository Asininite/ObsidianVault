### **Topic 4: Bitcoin Fundamentals (Keys, Addresses, and Wallets)**

**The Concept (How to understand it):**

Bitcoin was introduced in 2008 by a pseudonymous person or group named Satoshi Nakamoto. It is the first fully decentralized digital currency. A key distinction to remember for your exams is that **Bitcoin** (with a capital 'B') refers to the network and protocol, while **bitcoin** (lowercase 'b') refers to the actual currency.

Because there is no bank, ownership is proven entirely through cryptography:

- **Private Key:** This is a randomly generated secret number (like a password). If you lose it, your money is gone forever. If someone else gets it, they can spend your money.
    
- **Public Key:** This is mathematically derived from your private key.
    
- **Bitcoin Address:** This is a shorter, hashed version of your public key. This is what you share with others (like a bank account number) so they can send you money.
    

**What is a Wallet?**

A major misconception is that wallets "store" your digital coins. They do not. The coins exist purely as records of transactions on the blockchain. A **wallet** is simply a piece of client software that securely stores your _keys_, calculates your balance by scanning the blockchain, and creates transactions for you.

### **PYQs on Topic 4**

This specific section has appeared multiple times in recent KTU exams as high-weightage questions. Here is exactly how to answer them.

**PYQ 1: What is a wallet? List and explain its various types. (May 2024 - 7 Marks / April 2025 - 3 Marks)**

- **Answer:**
    
    - **Definition (2 Marks):** A wallet is client software that manages a user's digital keys, addresses, and balances. It allows the user to interact with the blockchain network to send and receive cryptocurrency. It does not store actual coins, only the cryptographic keys.
        
    - **Types of Wallets (5 Marks):**
        
        1. **Hardware Wallets:** These are highly secure, physical, tamper-resistant devices (like USB drives) used to store private keys offline. They are immune to computer viruses that steal software wallets. Examples include Trezor and Ledger.
            
        2. **Online (Web) Wallets:** These store keys entirely online on a cloud-based server. They provide a convenient web interface for transactions but require the user to trust a third-party service provider with their security. An example is GreenAddress.
            
        3. **Mobile Wallets:** These are applications installed on mobile devices. They are highly practical for everyday physical store purchases and often utilize the smartphone's camera to scan QR codes for quick payments.
            
        4. **Paper Wallets:** A cold storage method where the public address and private key are printed onto a physical piece of paper, often in the form of QR codes.
            

**PYQ 2: Explain how a payment is sent using a Bitcoin network in terms of the user's perspective. (May 2024 - 7 Marks / April 2025 - 7 Marks)**

- **Answer:** _(To secure full marks, write these exact sequential steps as outlined in your syllabus)_
    
    1. **Request:** The payment process begins with the beneficiary sharing their Bitcoin address with the sender (via text, email, or a QR code). Alternatively, the sender manually initiates the transfer using that address.
        
    2. **Creation:** The sender utilizes their wallet software to create the transaction data structure, inputting the amount and the receiver's address, and signing it with their private key.
        
    3. **Broadcast:** The wallet software broadcasts the newly created transaction to the peer-to-peer Bitcoin network.
        
    4. **Pickup:** Miners listening to the network pick up this unconfirmed transaction and add it to their memory pool.
        
    5. **Verification:** The miners verify the transaction's legitimacy (checking digital signatures and ensuring the sender has enough unspent outputs).
        
    6. **Mining:** The verified transaction is bundled into a candidate block. Miners race to solve the cryptographic puzzle (PoW) to mine the block.
        
    7. **Finalization:** Once successfully mined, the new block is broadcast to all nodes on the network.
        
    8. **Confirmation:** Users typically wait for up to six block confirmations (where 6 subsequent blocks are added on top) before considering the transaction fully final and probabilistically impossible to revert.
