### **Topic 3: Transactions, Messages, and Gas**

**The Concept (How to understand it):**

We know EOAs trigger actions, and Contracts execute code. But how does this communication actually happen, and what stops a hacker from writing a smart contract with an "infinite loop" that crashes the entire global network?

**1. Transactions:**

- A transaction is a cryptographically signed data package sent from an **Externally Owned Account (EOA)** to another account.
    
- It contains: the recipient's address, the amount of Ether to transfer, an optional data payload (used to trigger contract functions), and the sender's signature.
    

**2. Messages (Internal Transactions):**

- Contracts cannot start transactions, but they _can_ talk to other contracts.
    
- A **message** is data and value passed from one Contract Account to another Contract Account. It is never broadcast to the whole network; it only exists within the EVM execution environment during a transaction.
    

**3. Gas (The Network Fuel):**

- **The Problem:** The EVM is "Turing-complete," meaning it can run complex logic like `WHILE` loops. If someone accidentally (or maliciously) writes an infinite loop, every node on the network would get stuck running it forever.
    
- **The Solution:** Ethereum charges a fee for _every single computational step_ a contract takes. This fee is called **Gas**.
    
- **How it works:** * **Gas Limit:** The absolute maximum amount of gas the sender is willing to use for this transaction. If the contract hits an infinite loop, it will run out of gas, stop executing, revert the state, and the sender loses the fee. This perfectly solves the infinite loop problem!
    
    - **Gas Price:** The amount of Ether (usually measured in _Gwei_) the sender is willing to pay _per unit_ of gas. Miners prioritize transactions with higher gas prices.
        
    - **Total Cost:** `Actual Gas Used × Gas Price`

### **PYQs on Topic 3**

Gas is one of the most frequently tested concepts in Module 5 because it represents Ethereum's primary architectural difference from Bitcoin.

**PYQ 1: What is the concept of Gas in Ethereum? Why is it necessary? (April 2025, June 2023 - 3 Marks)**

- **Answer:**
    
    - **Definition (1.5 Marks):** Gas is the unit measuring the computational effort required to execute specific operations on the Ethereum network. Users must pay for this computation using Ether.
        
    - **Necessity (1.5 Marks):** Gas is necessary to secure the network. By charging a fee for every operation, it prevents attackers from spamming the network with useless transactions and prevents the EVM from getting stuck in infinite loops (solving the Halting Problem), as the transaction will simply run out of gas and terminate.
        

**PYQ 2: Explain Gas Limit and Gas Price in Ethereum. (Oct 2023 - 3 Marks)**

- **Answer:**
    
    - **Gas Limit:** This is the maximum amount of gas units a user is willing to spend on a transaction. It acts as a safety mechanism; if the execution requires more computation than the limit, the transaction reverts, but the user only loses the gas they allocated.
        
    - **Gas Price:** This is the amount of Ether (specifically measured in Gwei) the user is willing to pay per single unit of gas. Miners prioritize picking up transactions that offer a higher gas price.
