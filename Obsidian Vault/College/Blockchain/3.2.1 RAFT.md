## **RAFT Consensus Algorithm — Detailed Explanation (Exam-Ready)**

RAFT is a **consensus algorithm designed to achieve agreement in distributed systems under crash fault conditions (CFT)**. It ensures that multiple nodes (servers) maintain the **same consistent state**, even in the presence of failures such as node crashes or network delays.

The key idea behind RAFT is to make consensus **easy to understand and implement**, unlike more complex algorithms like Paxos. RAFT achieves consensus by organizing nodes into roles and controlling how they interact over time.

---

## **1. Basic Concept of RAFT**

In a distributed system, multiple nodes maintain a replicated log (list of operations). The challenge is to ensure that:

- All nodes agree on the same sequence of operations
    
- Even if some nodes fail, the system continues to function correctly
    

RAFT solves this by:

- Electing a **leader**
    
- Having the leader manage all updates
    
- Replicating data to other nodes
    

Thus, instead of all nodes competing equally, RAFT introduces **structured coordination through a leader**.

---

## **2. Node Roles in RAFT**

Every node in RAFT can be in one of three states:

### **Follower**

- Passive state
    
- Responds to requests from leader or candidates
    
- Does not initiate actions on its own
    

### **Candidate**

- A node becomes candidate when it suspects leader failure
    
- Initiates election process
    
- Requests votes from other nodes
    

### **Leader**

- Central authority for coordination
    
- Handles all client requests
    
- Responsible for log replication
    

At any time, there is **only one leader in the system**.

---

## **3. Leader Election Process**

Leader election happens when:

- No leader exists
    
- The current leader fails or becomes unresponsive
    

Steps:

1. A follower times out (does not hear from leader)
    
2. It becomes a candidate
    
3. It sends **vote requests** to other nodes
    
4. Nodes vote for the candidate if:
    
    - They haven’t voted yet
        
    - Candidate’s log is up-to-date
        
5. If the candidate gets **majority votes**, it becomes leader
    

If no candidate gets majority:

- Election repeats
    

This ensures:

- Only one leader is chosen
    
- System avoids split-brain scenarios
    

---

## **4. Log Replication (Core Working of RAFT)**

Once a leader is elected, all operations go through it.

Steps:

1. Client sends request to leader
    
2. Leader appends the request to its log
    
3. Leader sends log entries to followers
    
4. Followers store the entries
    
5. Once **majority of followers acknowledge**,  
    → leader commits the entry
    
6. Leader informs followers to commit
    

This ensures:

- All nodes eventually have the same log
    
- Consistency across the system
    

---

## **5. Safety and Consistency**

RAFT ensures strong consistency using the following rules:

### **Leader Append-Only**

Leader never overwrites or deletes entries in its log

### **Log Matching Property**

If two logs have an entry with same index and term:  
→ all previous entries are identical

### **Majority Agreement**

A log entry is committed only if:  
→ majority of nodes agree

These rules guarantee that:

- No conflicting logs exist
    
- System remains consistent
    

---

## **6. Handling Failures**

RAFT is designed for **crash fault tolerance (CFT)**, meaning:

- Nodes may stop working
    
- Nodes may recover later
    

Handling:

- If leader crashes → new election happens
    
- If follower crashes → it catches up later using log replication
    
- If messages are delayed → system still eventually reaches consistency
    

---

## **7. Heartbeats (Maintaining Leadership)**

The leader continuously sends **heartbeat messages** to followers.

Purpose:

- Prevent followers from starting elections
    
- Confirm leader is alive
    

If followers stop receiving heartbeats:  
→ they assume leader has failed  
→ start election

---

## **8. Why RAFT is Important**

RAFT simplifies consensus by:

- Clearly separating leader election and log replication
    
- Making behavior predictable and structured
    
- Providing strong consistency guarantees
    

It is widely used in real-world systems like:

- Distributed databases
    
- Configuration management systems
    

---

## **9. Final Understanding**

RAFT achieves consensus by:

- Electing a single leader
    
- Having all updates go through the leader
    
- Replicating logs to followers
    
- Using majority agreement for commitment
    

---

RAFT is a leader-based consensus algorithm that ensures consistency in distributed systems by managing leader election, log replication, and fault tolerance. It simplifies consensus by structuring communication through a leader and ensures correctness using majority agreement and strict log consistency rules.
