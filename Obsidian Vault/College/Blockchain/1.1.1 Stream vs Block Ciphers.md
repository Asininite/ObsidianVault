### **Topic 1.1: Symmetric Cryptography (Stream vs. Block Ciphers)**

**The Concept (How to understand it):**

As we established, symmetric cryptography uses the exact same secret key to both lock (encrypt) and unlock (decrypt) the data. However, the way the algorithm actually applies this key to the data falls into two distinct categories:

**1. Stream Ciphers**

- **Mechanism:** These algorithms encrypt the data continuously, **one bit at a time** (bit-by-bit).
    
- **How it works:** It generates a continuous "keystream" from the secret key and combines it with the plaintext bits (usually using an XOR operation).
    
- **Speed:** Generally faster and requires less memory, making it ideal for continuous data streams like video or voice calls.
    
- **Examples:** RC4 and A5 are commonly used stream ciphers.
    

**2. Block Ciphers**

- **Mechanism:** These algorithms break the plaintext up into **blocks of a fixed length** (e.g., 64 bits or 128 bits) and encrypt the entire block all at once.
    
- **How it works:** It takes a chunk of data, applies multiple mathematical transformations to it using the key, and spits out a completely scrambled chunk of ciphertext. If the final chunk of data is smaller than the block size, "padding" (dummy data) is added to fill it out.
    
- **Speed:** Slower than stream ciphers but highly secure and excellent for encrypting files and databases.
    
- **Examples:** Data Encryption Standard (DES) and Advanced Encryption Standard (AES) are typical examples of block ciphers.

### **Expected Exam Question**

While the symmetric vs. asymmetric question is more common, KTU examiners can easily drop this as a 3-mark differentiation question.

**Q: Differentiate between Stream Ciphers and Block Ciphers. (3 Marks)**

- **Answer:** _(Draw this comparison table)_
    

|**Feature**|**Stream Cipher**|**Block Cipher**|
|---|---|---|
|**Processing Unit**|Encrypts data continuously on a **bit-by-bit** basis.|Breaks data into **fixed-length blocks** and encrypts block-by-block.|
|**Key Application**|Generates a keystream that is combined with the plaintext.|Applies complex transformations (substitution/permutation) to the whole block.|
|**Examples**|RC4, A5.|AES, DES.|
