### **Topic 1: The Consensus Problem & Types of Faults**

**The Concept (How to understand it):**

Blockchain is a "message-passing" distributed system. Because there is no central server, all the independent computers (nodes) must talk to each other to agree on what the true data is. This goal of reaching an agreement is called the **Consensus Problem**.

For a consensus algorithm to work, it must be **fault-tolerant**. This means the network must survive and continue to agree even if some computers fail or act maliciously. There are two categories of these faults:

1. **Crash Faults:** These are innocent, simple failures. A node might lose power, experience a network interruption, or a message might get lost or delayed in transit.
    
2. **Byzantine Faults:** These are malicious failures. A node intentionally acts like a traitor, changing data, forging information, and sending contradictory messages to different peers to confuse the network.
    

To explain Byzantine faults, computer scientists use the **Byzantine Generals Problem**. Imagine several generals surrounding a castle. They must all agree to either attack or retreat at the exact same time to win. If even one general is a traitor and sends fake messages to the others, the attack fails. Blockchain nodes face this exact same problem.

### **PYQs on Topic 1**

Here are the questions that have appeared in past KTU exams specifically on this introductory section, and how you should answer them.

**PYQ 1: Explain the need for consensus algorithms in blockchain technology. (June 2023 - 7 Marks)**

- **Answer:**
    
    - In a decentralized, message-passing distributed system like a blockchain, there is no central authority (like a bank server) to validate transactions or maintain the ledger.
        
    - Consensus algorithms are required to achieve a unified agreement among all participating nodes on the network regarding the single, final state of the data.
        
    - They are necessary to make the network **fault-tolerant**. The algorithm ensures the system continues to operate correctly and reach an agreement even if some nodes experience downtime (Crash faults) or act maliciously to disrupt the system (Byzantine faults).
        
    - They solve the Byzantine Generals problem by ensuring that all honest nodes act synchronously to append valid blocks, maintaining the immutability and security of the blockchain without a trusted third party.
        

**PYQ 2: What is the Byzantine generals problem, and how is it relevant to blockchain design? (Oct 2023 - 3 Marks)**

- **Answer:**  It is an analogy involving several generals who plan to attack a castle. To succeed, they must synchronously agree to either attack or retreat. However, some generals might be traitors who send conflicting messages to confuse the loyal generals.
    
    - **Relevance:** In blockchain design, this represents the fundamental challenge of reaching distributed consensus. The network must be able to agree on the valid state of the ledger even when some nodes are faulty or malicious (Byzantine nodes) and intentionally broadcast forged or contradictory data to peers.
        

**PYQ 3: What are crash fault tolerant algorithms? (June 2023 - Part of a 7 Mark Q)**

- **Answer:** * Crash fault-tolerant (CFT) algorithms are consensus mechanisms designed specifically to handle distributed networks where nodes may experience simple hardware or network failures.
    
    - They can reach an agreement even if nodes experience unexpected downtime, network divisions, or if messages are delayed, lost, or delivered out of order.
        
    - _Note:_ CFT algorithms do not protect against malicious nodes forging data (they only handle benign crashes).
        

**PYQ 4: If your blockchain network has 6 Byzantine nodes, what is the minimum number of nodes that are required to ensure Byzantine fault tolerance using PBFT protocol? (Oct 2023 - 3 Marks)**

- **Answer:**
    
    - To tolerate Byzantine faults in a network, the minimum number of total nodes required ($N$) is calculated using the formula:
        
        $N = 3F + 1$
        
        _(Where F is the number of faulty/malicious nodes)._
        
    - Given $F = 6$:
        
        $N = 3(6) + 1$
        
        $N = 18 + 1$
        
        $N = 19$
        
    - Therefore, a minimum of **19 nodes** are required to ensure Byzantine fault tolerance.
