### **What is Consensus?**
[cite_start]In a blockchain network, **consensus** is the process of agreement between distrusting nodes regarding the final state of data[cite: 392, 393]. [cite_start]Since there is no central authority (like a bank) to validate updates, the network relies on a **consensus mechanism**—a set of steps taken by nodes to agree on a proposed state or value[cite: 397, 398, 51].

---

### **Core Requirements**
For a consensus mechanism to be effective, it must meet these five criteria:
* [cite_start]**Agreement**: Every honest node in the network must decide on the same value[cite: 461].
* [cite_start]**Termination**: All honest nodes eventually reach a decision and finish the process[cite: 462].
* [cite_start]**Validity**: The agreed-upon value must have been proposed by at least one honest node[cite: 463].
* [cite_start]**Fault Tolerance**: The system must continue to function even if some nodes are malicious or fail (often called **Byzantine nodes**)[cite: 464].
* [cite_start]**Integrity**: No node can make a decision more than once in a single cycle[cite: 465].

---

### **Primary Categories of Consensus**
Consensus algorithms generally fall into two architectural buckets:

#### **1. Leader Election-Based (Lottery-Based)**
[cite_start]Nodes compete in a "lottery" to win the right to propose the next block[cite: 470]. [cite_start]This is used in fully decentralized, permissionless networks like Bitcoin[cite: 475, 476].
* [cite_start]**Scalability**: Highly scalable (can handle thousands of nodes)[cite: 480].
* [cite_start]**Performance**: Very slow due to the competition process[cite: 480].

#### **2. Byzantine Fault Tolerance (BFT)-Based**
[cite_start]A more traditional approach where nodes reach an agreement through rounds of voting[cite: 477]. [cite_start]It is typically used in consortium or permissioned blockchains where participants are known[cite: 478].
* [cite_start]**Scalability**: Does not scale well to many nodes[cite: 479].
* [cite_start]**Performance**: Very fast with a limited number of participants[cite: 479].

### **Why Consensus Matters for Security**
	Consensus ensures the **immutability** of the ledger. [cite_start]For example, in a distributed system, a hacker would typically need to control **over 51 percent** of the network nodes to successfully start an attack, which is extremely difficult to accomplish in a large, decentralized network[cite: 57, 58].
