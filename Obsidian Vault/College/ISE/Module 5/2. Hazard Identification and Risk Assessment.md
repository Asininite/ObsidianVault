

### **1. Collect Existing Information**

The first step is to gather available data on workplace hazards from both internal and external sources to avoid "reinventing the wheel"1.

- **Internal Sources:** Equipment manuals, Safety Data Sheets (SDS), self-inspection reports, records of previous injuries/illnesses, and worker compensation records 2.
    
- **External Sources:** Information from trade associations, labor unions, and government agencies (like the Department of Labour) 3.
    

### **2. Inspect the Workplace**

Hazards can appear over time as equipment wears out or housekeeping declines. Regular inspections are necessary to catch these issues 4.

- **What to Inspect:** All operations, equipment, work areas, and plant vehicles (e.g., forklifts).
    
- **Checklists:** Inspections typically look for general housekeeping issues, slip/trip hazards, electrical hazards, and fire protection deficiencies.
    
- **Documentation:** Inspections must be documented (photos/videos) to verify that hazardous conditions are later corrected.
    

### **3. Identify Health Hazards**

Health hazards are often less obvious than physical safety hazards because they can be invisible (e.g., gases) or have delayed effects 8.

- **Chemical Hazards:** Solvents, adhesives, paints, toxic dusts9.
    
- **Physical Hazards:** Excessive noise, heat, or radiation10.
    
- **Biological Hazards:** Infectious diseases, molds, or poisonous plants11.
    
- **Ergonomic Risks:** Heavy lifting, repetitive motion, and vibration12.
    

### **4. Conduct Incident Investigations**

Investigating accidents, injuries, illnesses, and "near misses" provides a clear indication of where hazards exist13.

- **Goal:** The purpose is to identify the **root causes** (program shortcomings) rather than just blaming individual errors14141414.
    
- **Procedure:** Investigations should involve a trained team of management and workers who ask "Why?" and "What led to the failure?" (e.g., Was the worker adequately trained? Was equipment maintained?) 15151515.
    

### **5. Identify Hazards for Emergency & Non-Routine Situations**

Plans must be developed for foreseeable emergencies and tasks that don't happen every day 16.

- **Emergencies:** Fires, explosions, chemical releases, structural collapses, and disease outbreaks17171717.
    
- **Non-Routine Tasks:** Maintenance, startup/shutdown activities, and infrequent repairs18181818.
    

### **6. Characterize and Prioritize Hazards**

Once hazards are identified, they must be evaluated to determine which ones pose the greatest risk and need immediate attention19.

- **Evaluation:** Assess the severity of potential outcomes, the likelihood of the event occurring, and the number of workers exposed20.
    
- **Prioritization:** Address the hazards presenting the greatest risk first, using interim control measures to protect workers until permanent solutions are implemented21.
