# **Topic: Chemical Hazards and Control**

In any industrial setting, workers are exposed to various substances. A **Chemical Hazard** is any substance that can cause physical harm (explosions, fires) or health harm (burns, toxicity) to people or the environment .

### **1. Types of Chemical Hazards**

Chemicals are generally categorized into two main groups based on the type of damage they cause :

#### **A. Health Hazards (Damage to the Body)**

These chemicals attack the body's systems.

- **Acute Toxicity:** Chemicals that cause damage immediately upon exposure (e.g., Hydrogen Sulfide).
    
- **Carcinogens:** Substances that cause cancer (e.g., Asbestos, Benzene). Even small amounts can be dangerous over time.
    
- **Irritants/Sensitizers:** Chemicals that cause redness, rashes, or inflammation (e.g., Chlorine).
    
- **Corrosives:** Chemicals that burn living tissue on contact (e.g., Sulfuric Acid).
    
- **Mutagens/Teratogens:** Chemicals that damage DNA or harm an unborn fetus.

#### **B. Physical Hazards (Damage to Property/Safety)**

These chemicals are dangerous because of their physical properties.

- **Explosives:** Substances capable of detonating or deflagrating (e.g., TNT).
    
- **Flammable:** Chemicals that catch fire easily (e.g., Gasoline).
    
- **Oxidizers:** Chemicals that release oxygen, making other fires burn hotter and faster (e.g., Peroxides).
    
- **Gases Under Pressure:** Cylinders that can explode if heated or damaged.

### **2. The Hierarchy of Control**

This is the "Golden Rule" of safety engineering. When you identify a chemical hazard, you cannot just hand someone a mask and walk away. You must follow a strict order of solutions, from most effective to least effective.

**The 6 Levels of Control (In Order):**

1. **Elimination (Most Effective):**
    
    - Remove the hazard entirely.
        
    - _Example:_ Stop using a chemical adhesive and use mechanical fasteners (screws/nails) instead.
        
2. **Substitution:**
    
    - Replace the dangerous chemical with a safer one.
        
    - _Example:_ Using water-based paint instead of solvent-based paint.
        
3. **Isolation:**
    
    - Separate the hazard from the people.
        
    - _Example:_ Storing large quantities of chemicals in a shed outside the main building, or building an enclosure around a chemical process .
        
4. **Engineering Controls:**
    
    - Use physical machines or designs to remove the hazard.
        
    - _Example:_ Installing **Local Exhaust Ventilation (Fume Hoods)** to suck toxic vapors away from the worker's face .
        
5. **Administrative Controls:**
    
    - Change _how_ people work through rules and training.
        
    - _Example:_ Rotating workers so they are exposed for less time (reducing duration), or implementing Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) .
        
6. **Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) (Least Effective):**
    
    - This is the "Last Resort" because if the PPE fails, the worker gets hurt.
        
    - _Example:_ Respirators, chemical-resistant gloves, goggles, and aprons .

### **3. Hazardous Properties of Chemicals**

For the exam, you may be asked to define specific properties. Here are the key terms :

- **Toxic:** A substance that is poisonous. It can be hazardous (cause fire) AND toxic (poison you). Example: Gasoline causes burns/vomiting (toxic) and explodes (hazardous).
    
- **Explosive:** A substance that produces a rapid expansion of gas and pressure.
    
    - _Detonating:_ High explosives (supersonic shockwave).
        
    - _Deflagrating:_ Low explosives (rapid burning, like gunpowder).
        
- **Flammable:** A chemical that ignites easily.
    
- **Self-Reactive:** Thermally unstable chemicals that can decompose/explode even without oxygen.
    
- **Corrosive:** Chemicals that destroy materials and body tissue (Acids and Bases).

### **4. Material Safety Data Sheets (MSDS)**

This is one of the most critical documents in chemical safety.

- **Definition:** An MSDS is a mandatory document provided by the manufacturer that contains detailed information on the potential hazards (health, fire, reactivity, environmental) of a chemical product.
    
- **Purpose:** It ensures the **"Right to Know."** Workers and emergency responders must know what they are handling to stay safe. It provides far more detail than a simple label on a bottle.
    

**Key Contents of an MSDS:** An MSDS typically typically contains **16 Sections**. You should remember the most critical ones for the exam :

1. **Product Information:** Name of chemical, manufacturer contact.
    
2. **Hazard Identification:** Warning symbols (flame, skull) and risk statements.
    
3. **First Aid Measures:** What to do if inhaled, swallowed, or splashed in eyes.
    
4. **Fire Fighting Measures:** Which extinguisher to use (Water? Foam? CO2?).
    
5. **Accidental Release Measures:** How to clean up a spill safely.
    
6. **Handling and Storage:** Temperature limits, incompatible chemicals (e.g., don't store oxidizers with fuels).
    
7. **Exposure Controls/PPE:** What gloves or masks are required.
    
8. **Physical/Chemical Properties:** Boiling point, flash point, vapor pressure.
    
9. **Stability and Reactivity:** What causes it to react dangerously.
    

**Why is MSDS Mandatory?** It is the essential starting point for any safety program. Without it, employers cannot train workers, choose the right PPE, or plan for emergencies, leading to increased illness and injury .
