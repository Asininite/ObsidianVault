#### **What is PHA?**

Preliminary Hazard Analysis (PHA) is usually the **first attempt** in the system safety process to identify and categorize hazards or potential hazards associated with the operation of a proposed system, process, or procedure.

- It is a **semi-quantitative analysis** performed to identify potential hazards and accidental events that could lead to an accident.
    
- It relies heavily on **brainstorming** and expert judgment to assess the significance of hazards and assign a ranking to each situation.
    

#### **When is it Used?**

- **Timing:** It is used in the **early stages** of system design (conceptual phase) when detailed design data is not yet available.
    
- **Life Cycle:** It should continue throughout the system or product's life cycle to identify events that require more detailed risk analysis later.
    

#### **Purpose and Objectives**

- **Early Detection:** The primary goal is to identify hazards early so that design changes can be made when they are less expensive and easier to implement.
    
- **Ranking:** It provides a qualitative ranking of hazardous situations, which helps prioritize recommendations for reducing or eliminating hazards.
    
- **Cost Saving:** By identifying weaknesses early, PHA aims to save time and money that would be required for a major redesign if hazards were discovered later.
    

#### **Procedure for Conducting a PHA**

The procedure generally consists of four main steps :

1. **Define the System:**
    
    - Specify and clearly define the boundaries of the activity or system (e.g., does it include support systems like power and air?).
        
    - Define the intended functions of the system.
        
2. **Define Accident Categories:**
    
    - Specify the problems of interest, such as safety problems (injury), environmental issues (spills), or economic impacts (damage costs) .
        
    - Establish **accident severity categories** to prioritize resources.
        
3. **Conduct Review:**
    
    - Identify major hazards and associated accidents (e.g., fire, explosion, toxic release).
        
    - Identify hazardous equipment (e.g., fuels, explosives) and environmental factors (e.g., vibration, extreme temps) .
        
    - Identify design criteria or alternatives to eliminate or reduce these hazards.
        
4. **Use Results in Decision Making:**
    
    - Evaluate recommendations and determine if the risks are acceptable.
        
    - Identify opportunities for improvement (equipment mods, procedural changes) .
        

#### **Advantages vs. Disadvantages**

- **Advantages:** Helps ensure safety, modifications are cheaper early on, and decreases design time by reducing "surprises" later .
    
- **Disadvantages:** Hazards must be foreseen by analysts (if they miss it, it's not analyzed), and the effects of interactions between hazards are not easily recognized .
