### **Topic 1 (Revised): Needs for Safety & Key Definitions**

#### The Need for Safety

The importance of industrial safety is highlighted by the millions of occupational accidents that occur annually, resulting in a massive loss of production time1. This loss is not just from the injured individuals, who account for about one-fifth of the lost time due to disablement, but also from fellow workers who stop to help the injured and deal with any damage caused by the accident2. The economic impact becomes even more alarming when fatalities are considered3. Therefore, it is essential to identify, examine, and control the causes of these accidents4.

Several disciplines contribute to this safety approach5:

- **Industrial Engineering**: Deals with the design of efficient and safe workplaces, equipment, and layouts6.
    
- **Other Disciplines**: Psychology, sociology, and Medicare science also contribute to creating a safe working environment7.
    

To create a safe working environment, two main steps are taken8:

1. **Elimination**: If possible, eliminate the causes of accidents entirely9.
    
2. **Protection**: If elimination is not possible, arrange to shield the hazardous area using guards, enclosures, or similar protective measures10.
    

The need for safety is also driven by both direct and indirect costs of an accident. Indirect costs can include machine downtime, damage to equipment, idle time of nearby machinery, the negative psychological impact (horror) on other workers, compensation payments, and legal implications11. Implementing safety measures not only avoids these costs but also fulfills an organization's moral responsibility to its workers12.

Ultimately, the need for safety is to13:

- Protect the
    
    **health** of all employees14.
    
- Ensure the
    
    **well-being** of all workers15.
    
- Promote a
    
    **positive culture** in the organization16.
    
- Ensure
    
    **accident and incident prevention**17.
    
- Ensure
    
    **organizational development** and growth18.
    
- Ensure
    
    **continual improvement** of the company19.

#### Key Safety Definitions

**1. Accident**

- **Definition**: An accident is an unintentionally-caused event 20or an unexpected event that results in serious injury or illness to an employee and may also result in property damage21. Accidents in the workplace are referred to as
    
    **occupational accidents**22. The resulting harm may be called an "occupational injury" or "occupational death"23. There is no single universal criterion for what is considered an occupational accident24.
    
- **Costs**: Work-related accidents impose significant costs on the economy, including direct costs (property damage, lost worker hours) and indirect costs (decreased productivity from lower worker morale)25. Businesses may also face compensation costs, fines, and increased insurance premiums26.
    
- **Related Concepts**:
    
    - A
        
        **"near miss"** is an event that does not cause harm but had the potential to do so27.
        
    - An
        
        **"undesired circumstance," "unsafe condition,"** or **"unsafe act"** is a situation that could lead to an injurious or fatal accident28.
        
- **Causes**: Accidents can be caused by two main categories of factors:
    
    - **Human (Behavioral) Factors**: Including unsafe conduct, inattention, negligence, improper training, inexperience, drowsiness, fatigue, or illness29292929.
        
    - **Environmental & Workplace Design Factors**: Including unsafe working conditions, unsafe workplace design, substandard safety controls, and inclement weather30303030.
        

**2. Injury**

- **Definition**: An injury is an abnormal condition or disorder31. It is defined as "mental or physical harm to an employee caused by accident or disease"32. This includes damage to the body from accidents, falls, hits, or weapons33. It is specifically any physiological damage to the body caused by immediate physical stress34.
    
- **Types**: It can be caused by blunt trauma, penetrating trauma, burning, toxic exposure, asphyxiation, or overexertion35. Examples include cuts, fractures, sprains, and amputations36. The term also covers acute and chronic illnesses like skin disease, respiratory disorders, or poisoning37. It can also mean damage to artificial members, dental appliances, teeth, hearing aids, and eyeglasses (though for the latter two, only if the damage resulted from an accident that also caused a compensable personal injury)38.
    
- **Classification & Treatment**: Several major health organizations have established systems for classifying injuries39. Treatment is carried out by health professionals and varies greatly depending on the injury's nature40. Injuries are distinct from chronic conditions, psychological trauma, infections, or medical procedures, although an injury can contribute to them41.
    

**3. Unsafe Act**

- **Definition**: An unsafe act is a violation of an accepted safe procedure that could permit an accident to occur42. It is also defined as a situation where people violate standard rules and meet with an accident43.
    
- **Impact**: Studies attribute **80% of all on-duty injuries** to unsafe acts by people44. These are difficult to reduce as they revolve around human behavior45.
    
- **Examples**:
    
    - Operating a machine at an improper speed46.
        
    - Working without authority (e.g., entering a confined space before it's cleared)47.
        
    - Adjusting or lubricating moving machinery48.
        
    - Chance-taking (e.g., running in front of a forklift)49.
        
    - Not wearing required PPE50.
        
    - Failure to warn others of hazards51.
        
    - Improper loading or failure to secure a load52.
        
    - Making safety devices inoperable or removing them53.
        
    - Using defective equipment or using equipment improperly54.
        
    - Improper placement of work, improper lifting, horseplay, and servicing equipment while in operation55555555.
        
    - Failure to follow rules56.
        
- **Prevention**:
    
    - Think about how to do a task safely before starting57.
        
    - Warn coworkers of hazards, such as spills or defective equipment58.
        
    - Comply with all work rules and procedures59.
        
    - Do not use equipment you are not trained to use60.
        
    - Don't rush or take shortcuts61.
        
    - Use all tools and materials for their intended purpose62.
        
    - Use proper lifting techniques or get assistance63.
        
    - Use appropriate PPE64.
        
    - Stop and mentor employees engaged in unsafe acts65.
        
    - Supervisors must be vigilant in spotting unsafe acts66.
        

**4. Unsafe Condition**

- **Definition**: An unsafe condition is a hazardous physical condition or circumstance that could directly permit an accident to occur67. It is a work environment that is prone to having an accident68.
    
- **Impact**: Studies attribute **20% of all on-duty injuries** to unsafe conditions69. These relate to physical or mechanical defects that can often be corrected cheaply and permanently and are the first area to tackle in an accident reduction program70.
    
- **Examples**:
    
    - Inadequate or inefficient machine guarding71.
        
    - Defective conditions in hand tools, equipment, or substances72.
        
    - Poor layout, workflow, overcrowding, and congestion73737373.
        
    - Substandard housekeeping74.
        
    - Loud noise that prevents hearing instructions75.
        
    - Inadequate illumination or ventilation76.
        
    - Inadequate, improper, or defective protective equipment, tools, or materials77777777.
        
    - Inadequate warning systems78.
        
    - Hazardous environmental conditions like gases, dust, fumes, extreme temperatures, or noise exposures79.
        
- **Prevention**:
    
    - Think about the task and ensure you have all appropriate equipment before starting80.
        
    - Inspect all equipment, tools, and materials before use to ensure they are in good working condition81.
        
    - Make sure there is enough space to complete the job82.
        
    - If you identify an unsafe condition, cease the activity and report it to your immediate supervisor for corrective action83.
        

**5. Dangerous Occurrence**

- **Definition**: These are certain unintended, specified events that may not result in a reportable injury but have the potential to cause significant harm84.
    
- **Examples**: These incidents typically involve85:
    
    - Lifting equipment 86and pressure systems87.
        
    - Overhead electric lines88.
        
    - Electrical incidents causing explosion or fire89.
        
    - Explosions, biological agents, radiation generators, and radiography90.
        
    - Issues with breathing apparatus 91or diving operations92.
        
    - Collapse of scaffolding93.
        
    - Train collisions, wells, and issues with pipelines or pipeline works94.
        

**6. Reportable Accidents / Injury**

- The `MCN401 M1` document does not provide a specific definition for this term. However, the
    
    `MCN401 M2` document defines a **Reportable Disabling Injury** as: "An injury causing death or disablement to an extent as prescribed by the relevant statute"95.
