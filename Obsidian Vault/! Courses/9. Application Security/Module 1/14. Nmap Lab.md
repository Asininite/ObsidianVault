**Estimated time needed:** 20 minutes

## About This Lab
In this lab, you will learn how to scan a network with the domain name and/or IP Address using the ZenMap tool.

## Objectives
After completing this lab, you will be able to:
1. Use ZenMap, the GUI utility, provided by NMap
1. Perform a network scan based on the IP Address or domain name
1. Review different scan options in the ZenMap utility


## Important Notices about This Lab
### About Lab Sessions  
 
Lab sessions are not persisted. This means that every time you connect to this lab, a new environment is created for you. Any data or files you saved in a previous session are no longer available. To avoid losing your data, plan to complete these tasks in a single session. <br>

::page{title="Task 2 NMap with Zenmap"}

1. Click to open the Zenmap app on your desktop.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/JOQ-zJa2ZHwsUObblGH3qw/nmap-start1.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

2. This opens the Zenmap application.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/QA113yKcyqNMW8caMXmHVA/T2S2.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

3. In the **Target** field, enter **scanme.nmap.org**. This routes to your local system.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/OUq2ZB_arhq_0FRrEADuaA/T2S3.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

4. Choose **Quick Scan** from the scan options.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/X3Q3-hvRVA12SdmHfC9GDQ/T2S4.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

5. Click **Scan** to begin the scan process.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/_9zm0bATxMoYv77FBIsCsA/T2S5.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

6. You can see the output of the scan in the first **Nmap Output** scan results tab.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/m9GPu3ODdPaDiujS-fbL_Q/T2S6.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

7. Now from the list of scan options choose **Intense scan**. This is a more intense scan and gives detailed results. In realtime this will take a few minutes. Click **Scan** to begin intense scanning.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/crO-7fL3xXqfo9Et8nCFFA/T2S7.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

8. Once the scan finishes, you will see the detailed output along with how long the scanning took in the NMap output tab.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/qj7kmy7MAS6O5WpCsWr_wg/T2S8.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">


9. Click the **Ports/Hosts** tab to see state the ports in the target system. The **green** indicates open ports and **red** indicates closed ports.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/Jfdtx74JksHKF9Cmypcqhw/T2S9.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

10. Click the **Topology** tab to view the visualization of the hosts on this network. If a host has less than 3 ports, it will be green. If it has 3 to 5 ports it will be yellow. If it has more  6, it will be red. This will be evident when you test on real networks.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/ZQOlkBjuOzuNZu5YLTRIGQ/T2S10.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

11. Click the **Host Details** tab to get the details about the host you are scanning. The details will include the Host status, Address, Hostname, Operating system, and so on.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/4WJXWbe3535fpXr_fHrh0Q/T2S11.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

12. Change the target to 127.0.0.1 which is the IP address for your localhost and click **Scan**.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/_seL1jn2_o1LmVBOi1WGdA/T2S12.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

13. See the Nmap output of 127.0.0.1.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/EOQYLLd-DiC5wuZxzfZdLQ/T2S13.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

14. Change the target to cloud.ibm.com. and click **Scan**.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/VyuTL5UDJkSbK8m8SUDh4w/T2S14.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

15. See the Nmap output of cloud.ibm.com.
<img src="https://cf-courses-data.s3.us.cloud-object-storage.appdomain.cloud/O8KcuenWOFA9NU1o31MYIg/T2S15.png" style="display: block;margin: 1cm;width: 60%; border-style:solid; border-width:1px;">

Congratulations! Now you are familiar with using NMap to scan networks.
